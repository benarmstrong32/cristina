# Creative angles and hooks

## Awareness-level framework

Write to the viewer's actual state of mind, not a generic pitch. For any client, sketch at least one angle per row that applies to their business:

| Awareness level | What they know | What the ad needs to do | Example line (generic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Doesn't know they have the problem | Name the problem vividly, no pitch yet | "You've eaten dinner standing over the sink three times this week." |
| Problem-aware | Knows the problem, not the solution category | Introduce the solution category | "There's a reason you don't have to choose between takeout and cooking." |
| Solution-aware | Knows the category, hasn't picked a brand | Differentiate against alternatives | "Unlike [category], no subscription, no commitment." |
| Product-aware | Knows the brand, hasn't bought | Remove the last objection (price, trust, logistics) | "First order ships free — see what's actually in the box." |
| Most-aware | Has bought before | Reason to come back now | "This week's menu just dropped." |

A single-angle account is almost always stuck writing product-aware/most-aware copy to an audience that's mostly unaware or problem-aware. If a client's ads all read like "Order now! 20% off!", that's the tell — go up a level.

## Angle worksheet (do this before scripting anything)

For the offer at hand, fill in 3-5 rows minimum:

1. Angle name (one phrase)
2. Awareness level it targets
3. Core emotional driver (relief, status, fear of missing out, curiosity, trust/safety, convenience...)
4. The one claim this angle has to prove
5. Best format for this angle (UGC talking-head, process/build video, static offer image, testimonial, before/after)

Only after this worksheet is filled in do you write scripts or shot lists — scripting first collapses everything into whatever angle is top of mind.

## Hook-Value-Body-CTA (H-V-B-C) structure

Every video ad script follows this shape. Treat the hook as disproportionately important — it decides whether anyone sees the rest.

**Hook (0–3 seconds)**
- A pattern interrupt: visually unexpected, emotionally charged, or opens a loop the viewer needs closed.
- Never say the brand name here — it's the single fastest way to read as "ad" and get scrolled past.
- Good hook shapes:
  - Direct callout of the viewer's situation: "If you [specific behavior/pain], watch this."
  - Contrarian/pattern-break claim: "Everyone tells you to [common advice]. Don't."
  - Visual surprise tied to the product category, shown before any words.
  - Open loop: start mid-action or mid-sentence so the viewer needs the next few seconds to make sense of it.
  - Specific number or timeframe: "I stopped doing [pain] in 9 days."
- Target: hook rate (% who watch past 3 seconds) above ~35% before you call a creative ready to scale. Below that, the ad isn't earning distribution regardless of what follows.

**Value / Validation (3–15 seconds)**
- Prove the claim the hook made. This is where trust gets built or lost.
- Strongest proof forms, roughly in order of strength for cold audiences: real customer testimonial > visible demo/process > specific before-after > founder explanation > generic voiceover claim.
- Target: hold rate (% who watch past 15 seconds) above ~15%.

**Body**
- The fuller pitch: what it is, why it's different, what's included, what removes the remaining objection (price, effort, risk, trust).
- Keep it as short as the offer allows — this is not the place to introduce a new claim, only to support the one from the hook.

**CTA**
- One specific next action, stated plainly ("Order tonight's menu," "See this week's meals," "Get your first box free") — not a vague "Learn more" unless the funnel stage genuinely calls for it (unaware/problem-aware angles can end on curiosity rather than a hard CTA).

Hook-through performance correlates with final ROAS more strongly than the creator, the product, or the offer individually — if an account is underperforming and everything else looks reasonable, the hook is the first place to look.

## Hook-line bank (adapt, don't copy verbatim)

- "POV: it's 6pm and you still don't know what's for dinner." (unaware/problem-aware, visual open-loop)
- "I used to spend $[X]/week on [old habit]. Here's what I do now." (solution-aware, specific-number)
- "Nobody tells you this about [category]..." (contrarian)
- "Watch what's actually inside." (product demo, visual surprise, works well as a process/build shot)
- "This is the last time I'm ordering from [competitor category, not a named competitor]." (contrarian + problem-aware)
- "3 things I wish I knew before I started [behavior related to the problem]." (listicle open loop)

## Testing discipline

- Decide the primary metric and the pass/fail threshold *before* launching a creative test, and write it down. Judging winners after the fact invites picking whichever ad you already liked.
- Test angles against each other first; only test hook/visual variants of an angle once the angle itself has proven out — otherwise you can't tell whether a loss was the angle or the execution.
- Plan for volume: refresh hooks continuously (many accounts cycle 20-50 hook variants over a quarter against a handful of proven angles) rather than treating creative as a one-time production task.
