# Local/geo-radius targeting and retention strategy

## When a tight geo-radius fights Advantage+

Advantage+ Audience generally wants room to explore a broad pool and find who converts. A single-location or tight-delivery-radius local business doesn't have that room — the AI can run out of eligible people inside a 5-mile circle and either underdeliver or, worse, quietly serve outside the boundary to fill impressions (a "1-mile" radius can end up delivering as far as 8 miles out if Meta can't fill it inside the line). For this category of client:

- Don't rely on a fully open Advantage+ Audience alone. Layer a dedicated, manually-scoped **geo + interest ad set** alongside it: the geo-radius matched to the business's real delivery/service area, plus interests relevant to how people discover that category (for meal delivery: interests like DoorDash, UberEats, meal-kit brands, "healthy eating" style pages).
- Prefer the **"living in"** location setting over **"living in or recently in"** for hyper-local delivery businesses — "recently in" pulls in commuters and travelers who happen to pass through the radius, which is location creep that quietly wastes spend on people who can't actually receive the delivery.
- **Monitor the location breakdown of actual delivery**, not just the radius you set — this is the only way to catch the AI over-delivering outside the boundary.
- Anchor local creative with visual/textual cues that build trust for a hyper-local audience: map pins, neighborhood or street names, "serving [City], [City], and [City]" overlays, local landmarks in b-roll. This does real work for click-through and perceived relevance, not just aesthetics.

## Typical working radius by category (starting points, not rules)

| Business type | Typical radius |
|---|---|
| Local restaurant / prepared-food delivery | 3–10 miles |
| Retail storefront | 5–15 miles |
| Home services (HVAC, plumbing, etc.) | 15–30 miles |

Always tune to the client's actual delivery zone or service map rather than defaulting to these — a business with a hard delivery-zone cutoff (e.g., a courier route) should match the ad radius to that real boundary, not a generic industry number.

## Retention: the layer most accounts under-invest in

If the client's revenue model is repeat/reorder without a forced subscription — most local food, meal delivery, and everyday consumer-goods businesses — most of the available upside from a Meta budget often comes from getting past purchasers to come back, not from finding new ones. This layer gets skipped constantly because acquisition is the default mental model for "running ads."

Set it up deliberately:

- **Its own campaign**, not a leftover slice of the acquisition budget — retention traffic behaves completely differently (much warmer, cheaper, higher intent) and deserves its own budget line and its own creative (reminder-style, "here's what's new," seasonal/menu-refresh angles rather than pure acquisition hooks).
- **Audience:** custom audience of past purchasers/site visitors, segmented by recency if volume allows (e.g., "purchased 14–45 days ago" as a standing reorder-reminder audience).
- **Creative:** most-aware-level angles from the awareness framework — new menu/product drops, seasonal offers, simple "it's been a while" reminders. These don't need the same scroll-stopping cold-audience hook; the relationship already exists.
- **Catalog/dynamic ads:** if the business has a product feed, use dynamic catalog ads for this layer so returning visitors automatically see relevant items (what they bought before, what's newly added) without hand-building creative per SKU.

Treating retention as a first-class campaign rather than an afterthought is one of the highest-leverage, most commonly missed recommendations for this category of client.
