# Revenue and Funding Models for At Her Pace (atherpace.org)

Context from the local site (/home/user/cristina/site/index.html, read 2026-11-08): the site is titled "Resource guide" and describes itself as "an independent directory of public resources and practical information by U.S. state." Its creator is "a resource curator, not a licensed clinician, lawyer, or credentialed domestic violence advocate." The site says "At Her Pace is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by any organization listed here," and "Listings are not paid placements. No referral commissions or donations are collected through this site." Its privacy section says there are "no accounts, sign-ups, forms, cookies, analytics, or advertising trackers." **Nothing on the site says it is a nonprofit, a 501(c)(3), or fiscally sponsored. It does not provide direct services.** Both points shape every option below: most grants need 501(c)(3) status or a sponsor, and most federal DV money is for direct victim services.

## 1. Legal structure: 501(c)(3) vs fiscal sponsorship vs LLC/social enterprise

### Takeaway
At Her Pace has no legal entity that can receive charitable money. The fastest, cheapest route is a fiscal sponsor, which typically keeps about 5 to 15% of the funds it handles. The other route is incorporating as a nonprofit and filing for 501(c)(3) status: the IRS fee is $275 (Form 1023-EZ) or $600 (Form 1023), and approval takes a few weeks to many months. An LLC cannot take tax-deductible donations and is ineligible for nearly every grant listed here.

### Cited Findings
- IRS user fees: Form 1023 costs $600 and Form 1023-EZ (for small organizations) costs $275, both paid through Pay.gov. Fees can change, so confirm on IRS.gov. — [Inc Authority 2026 guide](https://www.incauthority.com/blog/how-to-apply-for-501c3-status/); [Wiss: 1023 vs 1023-EZ](https://wiss.com/form-1023-vs-1023-ez/)
- Processing time: the IRS publishes current processing times through its "Where's My Application" tool and says 1023-EZ cannot be expedited. Secondary sources estimate weeks for 1023-EZ (one says 80% are decided within 22 days) and 3 to 12 months for the full 1023. These figures conflict and are not official. — [IRS: Where's my application](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/wheres-my-application); [Inc Authority: timeline](https://www.incauthority.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-get-501c3-status/)
- Fiscal sponsor fees: many sponsors charge a flat percentage, typically 5 to 15%. — [Givebutter: fiscal sponsorship](https://givebutter.com/blog/fiscal-sponsorship)
- Model A (comprehensive) sponsorship averages a 12% administrative fee, according to a field scan cited by OpenGrants. The page carries an odd "verified" date, so treat the figure with caution. — [OpenGrants: fiscal sponsorship readiness](https://opengrants.io/encyclopedia/readiness/fiscal-sponsorship/)
- Open Collective: the standard fiscal host fee is about 10%, with some hosts lower (for example 6%). Organization pricing changed in April 2026. — [Open Collective docs: fiscal hosts](https://docs.opencollective.com/help/fiscal-hosts/fiscal-hosts.md); [Open Collective pricing update](https://opencollective.com/ofico/updates/updated-pricing-for-organizations-designed-for-long-term-sustainability)
- Fractured Atlas charges about 8% but offers only Model C (grantor-grantee) sponsorship and operates online only. — [Fiscal Sponsor Directory: Fractured Atlas](https://fiscalsponsordirectory.org/fractured-atlas/); [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/fractured-atlas-productions/)
- Directory of fiscal sponsors searchable by mission area. — [Fiscal Sponsor Directory](https://fiscalsponsordirectory.org/facts-and-stats2/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended sequence:** (1) Find a fiscal sponsor whose mission covers DV, women's safety, or technology for the public good, ideally a state DV coalition or community foundation. That lets the project take tax-deductible gifts and apply to small foundation grants within weeks. (2) Incorporate as a state nonprofit and file a 1023-EZ only after there is a board of at least three unrelated people and a funding track record. Many states also charge incorporation and annual report fees, and some states require charitable solicitation registration; those costs were not researched here.
- 1023-EZ eligibility (projected gross receipts of $50k or less a year, assets of $250k or less) is commonly reported but I did not verify it from IRS text this session. Confirm with IRS Form 1023-EZ instructions.
- An LLC or social enterprise only makes sense if the project's main revenue will be paid B2B work, such as workplace training. Even then, a nonprofit can earn that revenue itself, subject to unrelated business income rules. For an anonymous-feeling safety resource, nonprofit status also builds trust.
- The site currently says "No referral commissions or donations are collected through this site." Adding any revenue requires updating that copy, the privacy page, and the independence statement so they stay accurate.

### Gaps
- State-specific incorporation and charitable-registration costs were not researched.
- I did not identify a specific DV-focused fiscal sponsor that is currently accepting projects.

## 2. Grants: federal, state, private foundation, corporate

### Takeaway
Federal DV funding (VAWA/OVW, FVPSA, VOCA) is large but almost entirely for direct-service providers, governments, tribes, and coalitions, and in 2025-2026 it was unstable: delays, cuts, and ideological restrictions in litigation. A small online directory with no direct services is realistically ineligible, or uncompetitive, for these as a prime applicant. The more realistic sources are small private grants ($1k to $25k), local community foundations, and subcontracts or partnerships with an eligible DV agency or coalition. Even flagship DV foundation programs (Mary Kay $20k, Allstate) mostly target shelters and coalitions.

### Cited Findings
**Federal / VAWA (OVW)**
- FY2026 OVW Rural Program (O-OVW-2026-172579): awards of $500,000 to $950,000, about 55 awards, $36M total, no cost share, due about July 24, 2026 (one aggregator says July 28). Eligible applicants: states, territories, tribes, units of local government, and nonprofit (public or private) entities. — [Simpler.Grants.gov listing](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/362636); [HigherGov](https://www.highergov.com/grant-opportunity/ovw-fiscal-year-2026-rural-domestic-violence-dating-violence-sexual-assault-a-362636/); [DOJ GovDelivery notice](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDOJ/bulletins/41a129c)
- VAWA DOJ programs received $713M in FY2024 (about 1.6% of DOJ funds). The President's budget proposed cutting them to $505M; the Senate proposed $720M and the House $640M for FY2026. OVW applications "were unavailable for months" in 2025, and nonprofits sued over restrictions that would bar aid to trans people or immigrants. — [WFAE, Nov 2025](https://www.wfae.org/politics/2025-11-02/without-the-federal-government-almost-no-money-exists-to-fight-domestic-violence)
- NNEDV's FY27 asks: VAWA $1.16B total, including $100M each for Transitional Housing and Legal Assistance and $100M for the Sexual Assault Services Program. — [NNEDV FY27 Appropriations Factsheet (Feb 2026)](https://nnedv.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Approps_Factsheet_FY27_final.pdf)

**FVPSA (HHS)**
- FVPSA is "the only federal funding source dedicated to domestic violence shelters and programs." NNEDV asks for $500M in FY27. — [NNEDV FY27 Factsheet](https://nnedv.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Approps_Factsheet_FY27_final.pdf)
- The FY2026 FVPSA amount is reported as $245M by one source and $230M by another. CRS had not yet reported a final figure. The sources conflict. — [Marnie Shiels Substack](https://marnieshiels.substack.com/p/hhs-appropriations-impacting-gender); [CRS R42838](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R42838); [NNEDV FY26 chart](https://nnedv.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/FY26-Approps-Chart-FULL-CHART.pdf)
- At least 70% of FVPSA shelter and services funding goes to states and territories by formula, and the state administering agency makes the subgrants. — [ACF OFVPS Notice of Standing Funding Opportunity 2024-2026](https://acf.gov/sites/default/files/documents/ofvps/OFVPS%20Notice%20of%20Standing%20Funding%20Opportunity%20States%20and%20Territories%202024%20-%202026.pdf); [ACF OFVPS Grants & Funding](https://acf.gov/ofvps/grants-funding)

**VOCA (DOJ OVC, through state administrators)**
- VOCA comes from federal criminal fines and penalties, not taxpayer dollars, and funds nearly 6,500 victim service organizations. From FY23 to FY24, VOCA funds fell by about $600M, causing an average 40% cut to state victim assistance grants on top of FY21 and FY22 cuts. The House passed the Crime Victims Fund Stabilization Act (H.R. 909) in January 2026, and it awaits the Senate. NNEDV asks for $2B in FY27. — [NNEDV FY27 Factsheet](https://nnedv.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Approps_Factsheet_FY27_final.pdf)
- VOCA subrecipient rules are in 28 CFR 94.112 (public or nonprofit, record of effective victim services, volunteer use, match). I could not fetch the text (eCFR blocked the request). — [eCFR 28 CFR Part 94](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-94)

**Broader funding climate**
- "No other private or public organization has financed anti-domestic violence work as broadly or as deeply" as the federal government. Less than 2% of US philanthropy goes to women's and girls' causes. NoVo Foundation, formerly the largest gender-based-violence donor, ended that initiative in 2020. The Fund To Prevent Sexual Assault's new open call focuses on tech solutions, not direct services. — [WFAE](https://www.wfae.org/politics/2025-11-02/without-the-federal-government-almost-no-money-exists-to-fight-domestic-violence)

**Private and corporate foundations**
- Mary Kay Ash Foundation shelter grants: $20,000 each, unrestricted or operating use allowed. The 2026 round awarded 51 grants totaling $1,020,000, with at least one shelter per state that applies. More than 300 organizations applied, the deadline was about April 30, 2026, and recipients cannot win in back-to-back years. The program is for **shelters**. — [Rural Health Info Hub](https://www.ruralhealthinfo.org/funding/1176); [Dakota News Now, Oct 2026](https://www.dakotanewsnow.com/2026/10/15/working-against-violence-inc-wavi-receives-20000-grant-mary-kay-ash-foundation-support-domestic-violence-survivors-south-dakota/); [3BL Media: 30 years, $100M](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/30-years-100-million-countless-lives-changed-mary-kay-ash-foundation-expands-its-legacy-impact)
- Allstate Foundation (formerly "Purple Purse," now branded its domestic violence program): "Moving Ahead" grants go to **state DV coalitions** for financial-empowerment programs. One older round awarded $1.25M. The Purple Purse Challenge is an October fundraising competition (one listing gives an August 20, 2026 deadline). Coalitions such as Jane Doe Inc. regrant Allstate money locally. — [NCJA Connect2Grants](https://ncja.org/connect2grants-test-1/allstate-foundation---purple-purse-moving-ahead-grant-program); [The Grant Plant NM](https://www.thegrantplantnm.com/grant-detail/allstate-foundation-purple-purse-challenge/); [3BL Media $1.2M](https://www.3blmedia.com/news/allstate-foundation-awards-12-million-expand-programs-domestic-violence-and-financial-abuse); [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/allstate-foundation/)
- The Allstate Foundation is the largest recorded funder of the National DV Hotline in IRS 990 data: $1.3M across 23 grants (2021-2024). — [Impala profile](https://impala.digital/public/profiles/75-1658287/overview)

### Inferences
- **Realistic for At Her Pace now:** essentially no federal prime awards. Federal DV programs fund direct services, governments, and coalitions, and most require a demonstrated victim-services record. The fastest path to any federal or state money is as a **partner or contractor**: for example, a coalition paying the project to maintain its statewide resource directory, with funding that may come from VAWA, FVPSA, or VOCA.
- Realistic private sources, once sponsored or 501(c)(3): local community foundations (typically $1k to $25k), women's funds, tech-for-good funds (the Fund To Prevent Sexual Assault's tech focus is a possible fit), and state-coalition regrants. The Mary Kay shelter grant is not a fit because the project is not a shelter.
- Because of the 2025-2026 instability, even established DV agencies are scrambling for private money, so competition for small foundation grants is high.
- Accepting federal funding through a partner can carry VAWA or FVPSA confidentiality obligations and, in 2025-2026, contested grant conditions. Review grant terms carefully (see section 6).

### Gaps
- Avon Foundation (Speak Out Against Domestic Violence), Verizon (HopeLine, which was historically phone recycling), and Walmart Foundation DV-specific grant amounts and current 2026 status were not researched this session. Do not assume these programs are active.
- Typical local community foundation grant sizes were not sourced. The range above is an inference.
- FVPSA FY2026 final enacted figure is unconfirmed (sources conflict).

## 3. Individual donations

### Takeaway
Donations are the most realistic first revenue, but they need a tax-exempt recipient (a fiscal sponsor or a 501(c)(3)). Fee-free platforms like Zeffy cost the org nothing but pre-select a large donor "tip," and every platform adds third-party scripts and donor records that conflict with the site's zero-tracker promise. Host the donation page off the resource pages.

### Cited Findings
- Zeffy: 0% platform fee and $0 processing, funded by optional donor tips. One audit found a $16.95 (17%) tip pre-selected on a $100 gift. — [Donately Total Donor Cost](https://www.donately.com/total-donor-cost); [donationfeecalc: Zeffy vs Givebutter](https://donationfeecalc.pages.dev/zeffy-vs-givebutter/)
- Givebutter: 0% platform fee with tips on, 3% with tips off (about $6.20 per $100 card gift with tips off). — [donationfeecalc: Givebutter vs Donorbox](https://donationfeecalc.pages.dev/givebutter-vs-donorbox/)
- Donorbox: 2.95% platform fee on Standard, 1.75% on Pro ($150/mo), plus Stripe nonprofit processing of about 2.2% + $0.30 for cards and 0.8% (capped at $5) for ACH. — [donationfeecalc](https://donationfeecalc.pages.dev/givebutter-vs-donorbox/); [G2 comparison](https://www.g2.com/compare/donorbox-vs-zeffy-vs-givebutter)
- The fee site says it verified these rates against vendor pages on 2026-06-10. Confirm before choosing.
- The Allstate Purple Purse Challenge is an October (DV Awareness Month) fundraising competition. — [The Grant Plant NM](https://www.thegrantplantnm.com/grant-detail/allstate-foundation-purple-purse-challenge/)

### Inferences
- Practical playbook: a monthly recurring-donor ask (small, steady amounts suit a low-overhead website), an October DV Awareness Month campaign, and a Giving Tuesday campaign (in 2026, the Tuesday after US Thanksgiving, December 1). Peer-to-peer pages are best run by supporters on their own social channels, not from the resource pages.
- Donation pages should sit on a separate route (for example /support) that the resource pages do not load scripts from, and the page should disclose which processor sees donor data.
- Events (galas, 5Ks) take a lot of staff time relative to a one-person project. A virtual event or partnering with an existing community event is more feasible.

### Gaps
- No sourced Giving Tuesday or DV Awareness Month benchmark figures for small DV organizations.
- No sourced recurring-donor retention or average-gift data.

## 4. Earned revenue

### Takeaway
Earned options exist: workplace DV training, paid courses and workshops for advocates, speaking, sponsorships, merch, and Google Ad Grants (in-kind). Each one strains either credibility (the curator says she is not a credentialed advocate) or the site's privacy promise. Google Ad Grants needs 501(c)(3) status and mandatory conversion tracking. Ads and affiliate links are the worst fit. AmazonSmile no longer exists.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Ad Grants:** requires valid charity status and Google for Nonprofits verification. Ongoing rules: a 5% account-level click-through rate (two consecutive months below it means suspension), at least two sitelinks, geotargeting that matches where services are useful, conversion-based Smart Bidding, and **valid conversion tracking with at least one conversion per month** for accounts created on or after April 22, 2019. Accounts can be suspended without notice. — [Google Ad Grants eligibility](https://www.google.com/grants/eligibility/); [Google Ad Grants account management policy](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/117827)
- The $10,000/month figure is widely reported, but I could only confirm it from secondary sources this session. Government entities, hospitals, and academic institutions are excluded. — [Donorbox guide 2026](https://donorbox.org/nonprofit-blog/guide-to-google-ad-grants); [Getting Attention](https://gettingattention.org/google-grants-eligibility/)
- **AmazonSmile** shut down on February 20, 2023, and Charity Lists ended with it. The substitute is charity wish lists, which require supporters to buy items. — [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/Amazon-closing-Amazonsmile-to-focus-its-philanthropic-giving-to-programs-with-greater-impact); [Charity Navigator](https://www.charitynavigator.org/about-us/news-thought-leadership/end-of-amazonsmile/)
- **Workplace DV training market:** the leading national model, Workplaces Respond (Futures Without Violence), offers free model policies, training, and technical assistance, funded by DOJ OVW. Free competition for this service exists. — [Workplaces Respond](https://workplacesrespond.org/); [FUTURES: Workplaces Respond](https://futureswithoutviolence.org/workplace-safety-equity/workplaces-respond-to-domestic-and-sexual-violence)
- **Analytics and ads risk:** Safety Net (NNEDV) advises agencies to consider not using third-party analytics and to rely on server logs. If third-party tools are used, it says to minimize identifying data and tell visitors how to opt out. The core risk is that someone may discover a survivor was seeking help online. — [Safety Net: Agency website safety tips](https://www.techsafety.org/agency-website-safety-tips); [NNEDV Website Safety PDF](https://kaofeng-lee-s90t.squarespace.com/s/NNEDV_Website-Safety_2020-nwtf.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Google Ad Grants** is the biggest in-kind lever for a directory: free search ads for "domestic violence help [state]" queries. Its mandatory conversion tracking, however, means Google tags on at least the landing or conversion pages, which directly contradicts the site's "no analytics" promise. A possible approach (unverified with Google): count conversions on a separate, non-resource page (for example a "Get involved / volunteer" page or outbound click), keep resource pages tag-free, and disclose it in the privacy page. Weigh carefully.
- **Display ads, ad networks, and affiliate links:** do not use them on resource pages. They load third-party trackers and cookies, can leak "this browser visited a DV site" to ad profiles, can serve inappropriate ads (for example spyware or "track your partner" apps), and conflict with the site's "not paid placements, no referral commissions" independence promise.
- **Workplace training and consulting:** HR and employer-wellness buyers pay for training, but the curator lacks advocate credentials and free OVW-funded alternatives exist. Better: co-deliver with a credentialed local DV agency (revenue share), or sell a narrow product such as a "state-by-state employer resource kit" built from the directory data.
- **Courses for advocates:** credible only with credentialed co-authors. One possible angle is a paid "how to build and maintain a safe resource directory or website" course aimed at agencies, building on the site's own safety-by-design practice.
- **Licensing or maintenance contracts:** selling or licensing the curated, reviewed state directory to coalitions, hospitals, EAPs, or HR platforms (B2B; survivors never pay) is likely the most aligned earned option. No market data was found.
- **Sponsorships:** acceptable if limited to a "supported by" credit on an About or Support page. Never let sponsors influence listings or ordering. Sponsors from stalkerware-adjacent "family safety" or location-tracking businesses are a conflict. For example, DomesticShelters.org partnered with Aura, a consumer safety company (see section 5); review any such partner carefully.
- **Merch (purple ribbon):** low margin. Print-on-demand avoids inventory but puts a third-party store in the loop. Best run as an October campaign.

### Gaps
- No sourced pricing benchmarks for workplace DV training, keynote fees, or advocate course pricing.
- I did not fetch the official Google Ad Grants website policy or the $10k/month and $2 bid cap terms from Google directly.

## 5. How similar DV organizations and platforms fund themselves

### Takeaway
National DV platforms run on federal grants plus large corporate foundation gifts, or on a founding family foundation that later merges into a larger nonprofit. None of them relies on ads or charges survivors. A closely comparable model (DomesticShelters.org) began as a privately funded project of a family foundation and later became a program of a larger 501(c)(3).

### Cited Findings
- **The National DV Hotline:** its largest single funder is an annual federal FVPSA grant from HHS, renewed for services from October 1, 2025. Nearly three-quarters of its FY2024 revenue (over $20M) was federal. Revenue estimates range from $15M to $27.8M depending on the aggregator. The Allstate Foundation gave $1.3M across 23 grants (2021-2024). — [The Hotline: About](https://www.thehotline.org/about/); [The Hotline: HHS funding renewed](https://www.thehotline.org/news/hhs-funding-renewed/); [WFAE](https://www.wfae.org/politics/2025-11-02/without-the-federal-government-almost-no-money-exists-to-fight-domestic-violence); [Impala](https://impala.digital/public/profiles/75-1658287/overview)
- **DomesticShelters.org:** founded in 2014 by Arizona-based Theresa's Fund, merged into Alliance for HOPE International in 2023, and solicits donations to Theresa's Fund. It has corporate partnerships (Aura, a consumer safety company; Neon/IPG Health with the Out of Home Advertising Association) and runs shelter wish-list software used by 428 programs (2019). No revenue figures were found. — [DomesticShelters merges with Alliance for HOPE](https://www.domesticshelters.org/news/domesticshelters-org-merges-with-alliance-for-hope-intl); [Wish lists](https://www.domesticshelters.org/news/domesticshelters-org-begins-helping-shelters-fulfill-their-wish-lists); [DomesticShelters news](https://domesticshelters.org/news)
- **Futures Without Violence:** its workplace program is an OVW-funded public-private partnership offering free resources. — [Workplaces Respond](https://workplacesrespond.org/)

### Inferences
- The DomesticShelters.org path (family or private founding funding, then a merger into an established 501(c)(3)) is a real exit or sustainability option for At Her Pace: becoming a program of a coalition or national organization.
- Wish-list facilitation (helping shelters receive in-kind donations) is a non-extractive way to drive value without handling survivor data.

### Gaps
- One Love Foundation's funding mix (it was founded by the Love family after Yeardley Love's death and is known for corporate and campus partnerships) was not researched this session.
- Futures Without Violence overall revenue mix was not researched (check its Form 990 on ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer).
- Theresa's Fund and Alliance for HOPE 990 financials were not pulled.

## 6. Pitfalls specific to serving survivors

### Takeaway
The lines not to cross: never charge survivors or gate crisis information behind payment; never put trackers, ads, or affiliate scripts on resource pages; keep donor data minimal and segregated, since donors may themselves be survivors; and know that taking VAWA or FVPSA money, even through a subgrant, triggers strict federal confidentiality rules.

### Cited Findings
- VAWA confidentiality (34 U.S.C. 12291(b)(2)) applies to **all grantees and subgrantees** receiving DOJ VAWA funding. They "shall not disclose, reveal, or release" personally identifying information collected in connection with services requested, utilized, or denied. There are three narrow statutory exceptions (for example, informed, written, reasonably time-limited consent). — [OVW VAWA Confidentiality FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/ovw/page/file/1006896/download); [28 CFR 90.4 (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-90/subpart-A); [NIWAP OVW FAQ](https://niwaplibrary.wcl.american.edu/pubs/ovw-34-usc-12291-vawa-confidentlity-faq)
- FVPSA has parallel confidentiality requirements, and Safety Net explains how VAWA and FVPSA confidentiality interact with oversight and monitoring. — [Safety Net: Confidentiality in VAWA/FVPSA](https://www.techsafety.org/confidentiality-in-vawa-fvpsa); [Safety Net: VAWA privacy and oversight](https://www.techsafety.org/vawa-privacy-and-effective-oversight); [Comparison chart VAWA 2022 / VOCA / FVPSA](https://victimrights.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VAWA-2022-VOCA-FVPSA-Comparison-Chart.pdf)
- Safety Net website guidance: avoid third-party analytics where possible, minimize identifying data, and disclose any trackers. — [Safety Net: Agency website safety tips](https://www.techsafety.org/agency-website-safety-tips)
- 2025-2026 federal grant conditions restricting aid to trans and immigrant survivors are under litigation, and judges had sided with the nonprofits as of November 2025. — [WFAE](https://www.wfae.org/politics/2025-11-02/without-the-federal-government-almost-no-money-exists-to-fight-domestic-violence)
- Fee-free donation platforms default to large donor "tips" (17% on $100 at Zeffy), a trust and transparency issue. — [Donately audit](https://www.donately.com/total-donor-cost)

### Inferences
- **Do not charge survivors** for directory access, safety planning, or "premium" listings. Paid tiers for survivors would undermine the mission and could deter people in danger.
- **No paid placements in listings.** The site already promises this. Any sponsor or partner revenue must be structurally separate from which resources are shown and in what order.
- **Donor privacy:** donors to DV causes are often survivors or family members. Use a processor with minimal data collection, don't publish donor names without opt-in, avoid adding donors to ad-platform custom audiences (for example Meta or Google remarketing), and make receipt emails discreet (a neutral sender name and subject line).
- **Ad tracking:** any third-party pixel (Meta, Google Ads, affiliate networks) on resource pages could reveal DV-related browsing to ad profiles and shared devices. Keep revenue tooling on separate pages and update the privacy page whenever anything changes.
- **Federal money changes obligations:** if the project ever subcontracts under a VAWA- or FVPSA-funded coalition, the confidentiality rules and grant conditions flow down. Its current no-data design is well suited to compliance, but adding forms, accounts, or donor CRMs could create new PII obligations.
- **Credibility and scope:** the curator states she is not a credentialed advocate. Paid training or consulting sold under her own name risks overclaiming expertise. Partner with credentialed advocates.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Safety Net's specific guidance on donor privacy or donation-platform selection. No source was found that addresses the Meta pixel specifically for DV sites.
- VOCA's confidentiality provisions (28 CFR 94.115) were not retrieved.
