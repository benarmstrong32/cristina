# Private, Safe Online DV Peer Support / Survivor-Mentor Community for atherpace.org

Research date: 2026-11-08. US focus. About 22 search/fetch calls. Many vendor prices come from aggregators that disagree with each other. Discourse's prices come from its official page. Items marked "Inference" are design reasoning, not sourced fact.

## 1. Existing models and evidence for DV peer support

### Takeaway
Survivor-led peer support is well established in DV services, in person (e.g., SAFE Austin), in moderated forums (Women's Aid UK, Galop, Survivors.org) and in 1:1 mentoring (Brave Beginnings, Canada). The evidence is thin but promising. The best trial is MOSAIC (trained, supervised non-professional mentors), which showed reduced abuse scores. Systematic reviews say UK DV-service evidence is mostly high risk of bias and doesn't isolate peer support. The 7 Cups controversies show what goes wrong with lightly trained volunteers at scale.

### Cited Findings
**Forum models**
- Women's Aid (UK) Survivors' Forum: open only to women aged 18+ who have been affected by domestic abuse. It is for sharing experience and mutual support, "not a direct help service," and not for seeking advice for a third party — [Women's Aid Survivors' Forum guidelines](https://www.womensaid.org.uk/the-survivors-handbook/survivors-forum-guidelines/)
- Women's Aid tells users to assume anyone can read their posts and never to post their name, their children's names, workplace, location or contact details. Posts that could identify another user are banned. Links to blogs and promoting anything that could identify you are not allowed — [Women's Aid guidelines](https://www.womensaid.org.uk/the-survivors-handbook/survivors-forum-guidelines/)
- Women's Aid private messages are NOT moderated. Users are told to be careful about sharing identifying or court-case details, and to report abuse in a PM by screenshotting it and emailing the moderator. Moderator replies can take 48–72 hours. Off-topic or general chat threads are removed. Crisis users are pointed to the 24/7 National Domestic Abuse Helpline and 999 — [Women's Aid guidelines](https://www.womensaid.org.uk/the-survivors-handbook/survivors-forum-guidelines/); [search summary of forum guidance](https://www.womensaid.org.uk/?p=7990)
- Galop's LGBT+ domestic abuse forum uses pre-moderation: it may not approve a post, or may edit a post before it becomes visible. It also publishes a "keeping safe on the forum" guide — [Galop keeping safe on the forum](https://galop.org.uk/get-help/online-community/domestic-abuse-forum/topic/keeping-safe-on-the-forum); [Galop community guidelines](https://www.galop.org.uk/lgbt-survivors/forums/topic/community-guidelines/)
- Survivors.org (sexual-violence survivor community) moderates at the team's discretion, to protect survivors rather than suppress them. It deletes "potentially activating statements" and removes graphic violence or assault details. Guideline violations can end in account termination — [Survivors.org Community Guidelines](https://survivors.org/survivors-org-community-guidelines/)
- I couldn't retrieve Pandora's Project's own moderation rules (searches returned Survivors.org instead).

**Survivor-led / mentor programs**
- SAFE Alliance (Austin, TX) Survivor Peer Support is led by staff who are survivors. It offers survivor-facilitated groups, including identity-specific groups for queer, Black and Latinx survivors, plus individual peer counseling ("holding space, shared lived experience, strength-based feedback rather than clinical counseling"), system navigation, safety planning, and anonymous and social-media virtual support. It is free, and the only requirement is being a survivor. Its page doesn't describe training or supervision — [SAFE Austin Survivor Peer Support](https://safeaustin.org/our-services/face-to-face-support/survivor-peer-support)
- Brave Beginnings (Canada) pairs survivors with trained peer mentors for one year of 1:1 mentorship over Zoom. This is the closest analog to the atherpace concept — [Brave Beginnings](https://bravebeginnings.ca/what-we-do/) (via search summary)
- Wind Haven Foundation runs a virtual "domestic abuse peer recovery specialist" volunteer role with a certificate program. Its listing notes there is no national standard or state certification for DV survivor peer support — [Idealist listing](https://www.idealist.org/en/volunteer-opportunity/8634b8affd80451a87ea915dec058bea-domestic-abuse-peer-recovery-specialist-wind-haven-inc-muncie)
- Project S.A.V.E. (Philadelphia) is recruiting Certified Peer Specialists for remote, non-clinical, trauma-informed peer support for SA/IPV survivors, US-based only — [Idealist listing](https://www.idealist.org/en/volunteer-opportunity/60a27608ec68425a96f8cc8fd054bc76-certified-peer-specialists-join-the-survivor-support-network-at-project-save-project-savesurvivors-against-violence-efforts-philadelphia)
- NRCDV/VAWnet maintains a special collection on peer support and peer involvement. It also ran a 2024 webinar, "Strengthening Survivor-Led Peer Support in DV Programs," and hosts "Survivors Helping Survivors," a practical guide to peer support (SA focus) — [VAWnet peer support collection](https://vawnet.org/sc/peer-support-and-peer-involvement); [NRCDV webinar](https://vawnet.org/events/strengthening-survivor-led-peer-support-domestic-violence-programs); [Survivors Helping Survivors guide](https://vawnet.org/material/survivors-helping-survivors-practical-guide-understanding-peer-support-survivors-sexual)
- Other US online options listed in directories include isurvive (volunteer-run online abuse survivor group), Our Wave (recurring online DV groups, including Spanish), and Meetup online survivor groups. Their current status is not verified — [search summary incl. NSVRC directory](https://nsvrc.org/organizations/victim-survivor-support-organization?page=5)
- WomensLaw keeps a list of chats and message boards with a "keep safety in mind" warning. One listed group (YesICAN) says its chats are run by trained facilitators — [WomensLaw Chats and Message Boards](https://www.womenslaw.org/find-help/national/chats-and-message-boards)

**Evidence**
- MOSAIC cluster RCT (Melbourne, 106 primary-care clinics, 2006–07): trained, supervised local mothers made 12 months of weekly home visits, offering non-professional befriending, advocacy, parenting support and referrals. The abuse-score difference favored the intervention (15.9 vs 21.8; adjusted difference −8.67, CI −16.2 to −1.15). Evidence on the share of women with high abuse scores was weaker (AdjOR 0.47, CI 0.21–1.05). 76.4% completed follow-up. The authors called mentor support "promising" for safety and wellbeing — [Taft et al., BMC Public Health 2011;11:178](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/1471-2458-11-178.pdf); [protocol 2009](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/1471-2458-9-159.pdf)
- A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of UK DSVA support services found positive effects of psychological support interventions on self-esteem and apparent benefits from advocacy and outreach. Most studies had high risk of bias, and peer support was not isolated — [Carlisle et al., BMC Public Health (PMC11908015)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11908015/)
- A 2024 systematic review of training informal supporters (friends, family, community) found short-term gains in supporters' knowledge and attitudes. It is unknown whether victim-survivors actually find the resulting support helpful — [Schucan Bird et al., Trauma Violence & Abuse 2024](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/training-informal-supporters-to-improve-responses-to-victim-survi/)
- A Campbell evidence-and-gap map found 47 primary studies of interventions that build informal support for DVA victim-survivors — [search result summary, Campbell 2025](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/3965227)
- Qualitative studies of online forums: Reddit DV threads were "overwhelmingly positive" and offered help, advice and understanding, so forums "may help bridge the gap between survivors and resources." A study of three survivor forums found digital privacy and security advice being exchanged, and described technology as playing a "dual role" in IPV — [Online conversations about abuse](https://krimdok.uni-tuebingen.de/Record/1850777012?print=1); [JMIR 2023](https://jmir.org/2023/1/e42647/PDF); [London Met repository](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/1198)

**Cautionary peer-support platform: 7 Cups**
- Mashable's reporting (Rebecca Ruiz, 2024–25, seen via secondary coverage) found long-standing internal concerns about safety of minors and adults. It also found reliance on volunteers to moderate chat rooms and run groups, teens allowed to volunteer, and safety issues as a factor in losing a California state contract in 2019. In a 2018 criminal case, a 42-year-old man posed as a 15-year-old to get into the teen community — [AHCJ on Mashable investigation](https://healthjournalism.org/blog/2024/06/how-a-mashable-senior-reporter-exposed-hidden-dangers-of-online-teen-peer-support-forums/)
- Everyday Health (2024) found listener training "very brief and not highly regulated." Peer-to-peer platforms are not necessarily subject to FDA regulation or FTC enforcement — [search summaries, incl. ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/is-7-cups-legit/)
- 7 Cups created thousands of therapist profiles without consent, and messages sent to them were redirected to peer listeners — [Behavioral Health Business 2025](https://bhbusiness.com/2025/04/18/therapist-criticize-digital-health-app-7-cups-for-creating-profiles-without-permission/)

### Inferences
- Combine two of these models. Use a Women's Aid/Galop-style pre-moderated forum for many-to-many support. Use MOSAIC/Brave Beginnings-style structured 1:1 mentoring with trained, supervised mentors and a defined term (e.g., 3–12 months) for the "further along survivor mentors current seeker" piece.
- Avoid the Women's Aid "unmoderated PMs" gap. Either disable member-to-member DMs, or allow DMs only inside matched mentor pairs and log them for safety review with consent.
- Market it as "peer support, not therapy or advocacy," and don't claim proven outcomes. Say "promising evidence."

### Gaps
- I didn't fetch details on Refuge (UK), SafeLives Pioneers, RAINN's online hotline/peer offerings, The Hotline's peer partners, or Mental Health America's peer programs. For RAINN: its Online Hotline is staff/volunteer-run 1:1 chat rather than peer-to-peer (from background knowledge, unverified here).
- I found no RCT of online DV peer forums or online 1:1 survivor mentoring.

## 2. DV-specific safety risks and mitigations

### Takeaway
The threat model is an abuser (or an abuser's ally) who joins the community, monitors a survivor's device, or uses data the platform holds. Existing forums rely on pre-moderation, banning identifying details, assuming posts are public, and reporting via screenshot. NNEDV stresses that encryption does not protect a survivor whose device is monitored. I found no published method that reliably keeps abusers out. Verification is a trade-off rather than a solved problem.

### Cited Findings
- Abusers control survivors' support networks and can retaliate, which makes it "extremely challenging" for survivors to seek support. Survivors join online support groups using technology that carries these risks — [Gupta et al., USENIX Security 2024](https://usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity24-gupta.pdf)
- Abusers impersonate victims online and can use that to get others to harass or endanger the victim — [WomensLaw: impersonation](https://nocache.womenslaw.org/about-abuse/abuse-using-technology/ways-survivors-use-and-abusers-misuse-technology/impersonation)
- Real-world infiltration precedent: an adult man posed as a 15-year-old to get into 7 Cups' teen community (2018 criminal case) — [AHCJ](https://healthjournalism.org/blog/2024/06/how-a-mashable-senior-reporter-exposed-hidden-dangers-of-online-teen-peer-support-forums/)
- Encryption is not a full solution, because anyone with device access or stalkerware can still read messages — [NNEDV encryption resources / search summary](https://www.techsafety.org/understanding-encryption)
- Text threads saved on a phone can reveal that someone is seeking help — [NNEDV Choosing Digital Service Platforms](https://techsafety.org/step-by-step-guide)
- Under VAWA, PII includes IP addresses, email, phone, DOB and any information "likely to disclose the location of a victim." Combined data points and metadata can identify someone — [TechSafety VAWA privacy](https://www.techsafety.org/vawa-privacy-and-effective-oversight)
- Women's Aid rules: assume posts are public, no names, children's names, workplace or location, no court-case details, and no content that identifies others — [Women's Aid](https://www.womensaid.org.uk/the-survivors-handbook/survivors-forum-guidelines/)
- In May 2026 Instagram removed the end-to-end encryption option for DMs, so social-platform groups are a weak channel — [TechSafety encryption blog](https://www.techsafety.org/blog/tag/encryption)
- Safety Net (NNEDV) trains tech companies on how abusers misuse platforms and how to build safety features. It says survivors are a large share of mainstream users — [TechSafety For Technologists](https://www.techsafety.org/for-technologists)

### Inferences (design requirements)
- **Identity/vetting without exposure:** Use invite or referral-based admission plus a short live (video or voice) intake call with a trained staff member, run as a conversational safety screen rather than proof of ID. Don't collect government ID. Don't store more than an email address, which can be a fresh alias address. Option: accept a referral attestation from a partner DV agency or hotline advocate without receiving the case file. Newcomers start in a read-only "new member" tier with all posts pre-moderated for N weeks. Mentors get a much higher bar (see §3).
- **Pseudonyms:** Make pseudonyms mandatory. Block real-name-looking handles and default avatars only (no photo uploads, or strip EXIF/GPS if images are allowed). Hide member lists and "last seen/online" status. No public profiles and no search-engine indexing.
- **Screenshots:** These can't be prevented technically on the web. State in the rules and onboarding that anything posted may be screenshotted. Keep sensitive sub-spaces small, pre-moderated and mentor-only.
- **Location/metadata leakage:** Strip EXIF. Don't show timezones. Keep IP logs short (e.g., 7–30 days, or truncated). Make email notifications off by default or content-free ("You have a new message") so a monitored inbox reveals nothing. Use a neutral sender name and subject line.
- **Device monitoring:** Add a quick-exit button (Esc), a "cover your tracks" page, and a "check if your device is safe" guide linking to TechSafety. Use short session timeouts, no "remember me" by default, no push notifications, and a PWA or web app with no installed icon. Offer a discreet app name if a mobile app is ever built.
- **Abuser infiltration signals for moderators:** Watch for members asking others for location, court dates, shelter names or children's schools. Watch for members probing a specific story or pushing to move off-platform to DMs or phone. Hard rule: no moving off-platform, and no sharing of contact info.
- **Dual-membership risk:** Both partners may claim to be the victim. Policy: on a credible concern, staff quietly remove the account and keep no "explanations" on the record. Get legal advice on documentation.

### Gaps
- I found no published verification protocol used by a DV forum to screen out abusers. Contacting Galop, Women's Aid, or Safety Net directly is recommended.

## 3. Moderation, training, crisis escalation, mandated reporting, burnout

### Takeaway
There is no national certification for DV survivor peer supporters. The practical benchmark is the roughly 40-hour state DV advocate/counselor training (e.g., CA Evid. Code §1037.1, Oregon ORS 40.264), which also triggers advocate privilege in some states. Moderation needs paid or staff oversight, pre-moderation for newcomers, a crisis hand-off to the National DV Hotline/988/911, and clinical supervision for mentors.

### Cited Findings
- California defines a "domestic violence counselor" as someone with at least 40 hours of training, including the victim-counselor privilege, confidentiality laws, and peer counseling techniques — [CA Evid. Code §1037.1 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/evidence-code/evid-sect-1037-1/)
- Oregon "certified advocate" status requires at least 40 hours of AG-approved training plus a 2-hour confidentiality/privilege course. Privilege does not attach until the training is finished — [OCADSV](https://www.ocadsv.org/resources/confidentiality-protections-for-victims-and-certified-advocates/); [Oregon DOJ advocate privilege](https://www.doj.state.or.us/crime-victims/for-grantees/advocate-privilege-and-confidentiality-requirements/); [OR training review 2026](https://www.doj.state.or.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Training-Review-CVSSD-ODHS-OCADSV-MArch-2026-.pdf)
- Advocate privilege varies by state. Arizona's covers civil actions, while Alabama's is limited to criminal proceedings — [Confidentiality Institute privilege chart 2022](https://techsafety.org/s/CI_Advocate-Confidentiality-Privilege-laws_2022.pdf)
- Washington's Certified Peer Specialist track uses 80 hours of training plus a separate 40-hour crisis course. This is a behavioral-health credential, not a DV one — [WA HCA CPS FAQ](https://hca.wa.gov/assets/billers-and-providers/certified-peer-specialists-faq.pdf)
- No national standard or state certification exists for DV survivor peer support — [Wind Haven listing](https://www.idealist.org/en/volunteer-opportunity/8634b8affd80451a87ea915dec058bea-domestic-abuse-peer-recovery-specialist-wind-haven-inc-muncie)
- MOSAIC mentors were "trained and supervised," and that supervision is part of the model behind the positive result — [Taft et al. 2011](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/1471-2458-11-178.pdf)
- 7 Cups listeners complain of very little support when dealing with members with severe psychiatric issues, an example of burnout and under-support — [search summary incl. Trustpilot reviews](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.7cups.com)
- Survivors.org removes graphic violence and assault details, a trigger-management norm — [Survivors.org](https://survivors.org/survivors-org-community-guidelines/)

### Inferences (recommended program design)
- **Mentor eligibility:** At least 12–24 months out from the abusive relationship, or self-assessed stable, and currently safe. Reference from an advocate or another program is preferred. Complete a 40-hour DV advocate-equivalent curriculum (state coalition training or partner agency), plus atherpace-specific modules: boundaries, no advice on legal or custody matters, safety planning basics, the crisis protocol, tech safety, and self-care. Sign a confidentiality and code-of-conduct agreement. Get a background check only if mentors will meet in person or work with minors. For online-only adult work, weigh background-check data collection against survivor privacy.
- **Boundaries code:** No off-platform contact, money, housing offers or in-person meetings. Don't tell a mentee to leave or stay. Don't give legal or medical advice; refer instead. No romantic or sexual contact. No self-disclosure of identifying details. Cap each mentor's caseload (e.g., 1–3 mentees).
- **Crisis escalation:** Flag keywords in posts (suicide, "he's here," weapons, children in danger). Pin a crisis banner everywhere: National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233 / text START to 88788, 988 for suicide, 911. Peers hand off to the hotline and to staff, and never stay as sole support. Staff on-call coverage hours must be published. Outside those hours, say plainly "this space is not monitored 24/7."
- **Mandated reporting:** Peers who are not employees of a mandated profession are generally not mandated reporters of adult DV. Child abuse reporting laws vary by state, and some states (e.g., TX, NJ, and others) make every adult a mandated reporter. Disclose limits of confidentiality at intake. Have counsel write a state-by-state policy. (Unverified here; see Gaps.)
- **Supervision and vicarious trauma:** Monthly group supervision run by a licensed clinician (LCSW/LPC) or an experienced DV program manager. Required debriefs after crisis events. Mentor term limits and sabbaticals. A stipend or honorarium where possible.
- **Moderation staffing:** At least one paid moderator/coordinator. Pre-moderate all newcomer posts and all posts in mentor-match spaces. Moderators edit out identifying details (the Galop model). Volunteer survivor moderators may help, but final decisions sit with staff.

### Gaps
- I couldn't verify a state-by-state mandated-reporter list for this population. Use the Child Welfare Information Gateway "Mandatory Reporters" chart and counsel.
- I found no published vicarious-trauma rates for DV peer mentors specifically.

## 4. Platform and tech options (privacy, cost, E2E, HIPAA, retention)

### Takeaway
NNEDV's guidance is to evaluate platforms on vendor access to data, retention, encryption, configurability and data minimization. A HIPAA claim does not satisfy VAWA, and no tool is "VAWA compliant." Discourse (self-hosted or paid hosted) is the strongest fit for a private forum: open source, full data control, and fine-grained trust levels and pre-moderation. Circle and Mighty Networks are polished but are creator platforms that hold your data. Signal is good for small mentor-pair chats but exposes phone numbers unless usernames are used. Discord is a poor fit.

### Cited Findings
- NNEDV's platform criteria: does it offer end-to-end encryption, where data is stored (local or cloud), breach risk, whether the vendor can access video history or PII about who joined, whether the program can, whether chats are saved on device or cloud and whether that can change, configurable privacy settings, minimal data collection, and checking "HIPAA-compliant" claims against VAWA/FVPSA/VOCA. "No tool is labeled VAWA/FVPSA/VOCA-compliant" — [NNEDV Choosing Digital Service Platforms](https://techsafety.org/step-by-step-guide)
- NNEDV publishes a vendor-selection checklist and a Digital Services Toolkit — [Choosing a Vendor Checklist](https://www.techsafety.org/s/NNEDV_Choosing-a-Vendor-Checklist_2020.pdf); [Digital Services Toolkit](https://nnedv.org/latest_update/nnedv-launches-digital-services-toolkit/)
- HIPAA's business associate exception does not exist under VAWA/FVPSA/VOCA, so a vendor contract alone doesn't authorize vendor access to PII — [TechSafety VAWA privacy](https://www.techsafety.org/vawa-privacy-and-effective-oversight)
- Discourse (official pricing): open source and free to self-host. Hosted plans: Free (2 staff, 10 categories, 5GB), Pro $100/mo (5 staff, 20GB), Business $500/mo (15 staff, SSO via OpenID/OAuth2, 100GB), Enterprise custom (GDPR/CCPA-compliant hosting, dedicated AWS add-on). Unlimited members. Full browser-based backup and export — [Discourse pricing](https://www.discourse.org/pricing)
- Circle: aggregators put entry pricing at about $89/mo, up to $199/mo, with custom enterprise — [G2 Circle pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/circle-so-circle/pricing); [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/compare/circle-vs-discourse/)
- Mighty Networks: aggregators conflict. One says $79 Launch / $179 Scale / custom Pro, another $95/$215, another ~$41/mo annual, plus transaction fees — [Kourses](https://kourses.com/?p=11768); [SchoolMaker](https://www.schoolmaker.com/blog/mighty-networks-pricing); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/185819/Mighty-Networks/pricing/)
- Zoom (2020 NNEDV guidance): hosts may see chat history. A paid BAA tier (~$200/mo at the time) enabled E2E encrypted video and chat. Check current docs — [NNEDV Using Zoom](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/51dc541ce4b03ebab8c5c88c/t/5ede6a56e9fe5555c8b82aa2/1591634519185/Using+Zoom-Safety+Privacy+Conf+Considerations+FINAL.pdf); [NNEDV Communication Tools handout](https://niwaplibrary.wcl.american.edu/wp-content/uploads/NNEDV_CommunicationTools_Handout.pdf)

### Inferences (platform comparison; prices beyond Discourse need vendor confirmation)
| Option | Fit | Privacy notes | Approx cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discourse self-hosted** (recommended core) | Private, invite-only forum. Trust levels give a newcomer tier; approval queue gives pre-moderation; category-level permissions give a mentor-only space; built-in flagging | Full data control, can set log retention and IP anonymization; requires a sysadmin | Server ~$20–60/mo plus setup and maintenance labor |
| Discourse hosted Pro/Business | Same, without running servers | Discourse Inc. holds the data; vendor access issue under VAWA if VAWA-funded | $100 / $500 per month |
| Circle / Mighty Networks | Polished courses, events and mobile app | Commercial creator platforms; analytics-heavy; data on vendor; not built for DV threat model | ~$80–220/mo plus fees |
| Signal (groups / usernames) | Small 1:1 mentor pairs or tiny groups | E2E, disappearing messages; but on the survivor's phone (device-monitoring risk); phone numbers exposed unless username-only mode is used; hard to moderate or audit | Free |
| Discord | Not recommended | Data on Discord's servers, not E2E for text; persistent identities across servers; DMs from strangers; minors present; weak control over member discovery | Free |
| Zoom / Jitsi Meet (live groups) | Weekly facilitated groups | Zoom: enable waiting room, passcodes, no recording, E2E mode. Jitsi: open source, can self-host for no vendor logs. Require pseudonym display names; camera optional | Zoom ~$15–25/host/mo; Jitsi self-host server cost |
| Custom build | Only at scale | Highest control but highest security burden; not for v1 | $50k+ |

- **Data retention:** Auto-purge or anonymize IP logs after ≤30 days. Let members delete their account with posts anonymized (Women's Aid offers deletion on request). Keep no copies of intake notes beyond a minimal eligibility flag. Run quarterly backups encrypted, with short retention.
- **HIPAA:** Not applicable unless atherpace is a covered entity (it isn't, if no billing of health plans). Don't market as "HIPAA compliant." Frame as VAWA-style confidentiality practices.

## 5. Legal / liability, VAWA confidentiality, age limits, terms

### Takeaway
If atherpace (or a partner) takes VAWA, FVPSA or VOCA funds, strict non-disclosure of survivor PII applies. That covers vendors and funders, requires written, informed, time-limited consent, and survives encryption. Even without that funding, adopting these rules is best practice. Restrict to adults 18+ like Women's Aid. Use clear "peer support, not therapy, advocacy or emergency service" terms. Get insurance (general liability, professional/abuse-and-molestation, D&O, cyber) and legal review.

### Cited Findings
- VAWA/VOCA grantees may not disclose PII "regardless of whether the information has been encoded, encrypted, hashed." Sharing with anyone outside the victim services program counts as disclosure, including funders and vendors. Consent must be written, informed, time-limited and specific, and services can't be conditioned on it. Exceptions are a valid court mandate (a subpoena is not necessarily one) or a statutory mandate, with notice to the victim and steps to protect them. Programs should adopt a written non-disclosure policy — [TechSafety VAWA privacy](https://www.techsafety.org/vawa-privacy-and-effective-oversight); [TechSafety confidentiality in VAWA/FVPSA](https://www.techsafety.org/confidentiality-in-vawa-fvpsa)
- OVW FAQ: grant-funded service providers may not share PII even with other divisions of their own organization — [OVW VAWA confidentiality FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/ovw/page/file/1006896/download)
- PII statutory citation is 34 U.S.C. §12291(a)(20) per TechSafety. A 2025 OVC bulletin cites (a)(25), so verify current numbering — [search summary](https://www.techsafety.org/faq-federal-laws)
- Aggregate reporting must use broad categories, because small demographic groups can be re-identified — [TechSafety](https://www.techsafety.org/vawa-privacy-and-effective-oversight)
- Women's Aid sets adults (18+) only and women only — [Women's Aid](https://www.womensaid.org.uk/the-survivors-handbook/survivors-forum-guidelines/)
- Peer platforms fall outside FDA and FTC oversight, which leaves liability to platform policy — [ChoosingTherapy / Mashable summary](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/is-7-cups-legit/)
- Advocate privilege only attaches if the state statute's training and organizational definitions are met — [Confidentiality Institute chart](https://techsafety.org/s/CI_Advocate-Confidentiality-Privilege-laws_2022.pdf); [CA §1037.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/evidence-code/evid-sect-1037-1/)

### Inferences
- **Terms and disclaimers:** "Not therapy, legal advice, or an emergency service; not monitored 24/7." Peer mentors are volunteers, not counselors or advocates (unless state-certified). Users must be 18+. Content may be screenshotted. Users may be removed at the organization's discretion without explanation. Include a limitation of liability and a crisis resources banner.
- **Women-only policy:** Under US public accommodation law, an online nonprofit support group restricted by gender is generally defensible, but get counsel. Decide explicitly whether trans women are included, and say so. Consider LGBTQ+ survivors' needs and referrals (e.g., Galop-style spaces, the Northwest Network).
- **Insurance:** General liability, professional liability (covering volunteers), abuse and molestation (sexual misconduct), D&O, and cyber/privacy breach. Volunteer accident coverage if there are any events.
- **Structure:** Running under a 501(c)(3), or a fiscal sponsor, or in partnership with an established DV agency gives access to their training, supervision and insurance, and potentially advocate privilege.
- **Data subpoenas:** Minimal data retention is the strongest protection. Write a subpoena-response policy (contest, notify user).
- **Privacy law:** State privacy laws (e.g., Washington's My Health My Data Act, CCPA/CPRA) may cover health-adjacent data. Get counsel review.

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve primary sources on insurance costs, the Washington MHMDA's applicability, or state mandated-reporter laws.

## 6. Ethical monetization and phased launch plan

### Takeaway
I found no source on monetizing DV peer communities. The ethical norm in the field (SAFE Austin, Women's Aid, the hotlines) is free for survivors. Funding comes from grants, donors, sponsors and partner agencies, never ads, tracking pixels or selling data. Launch small (a pilot cohort with partner referral), then scale.

### Cited Findings
- Survivor services in the cited models are free with no means test (SAFE Austin: "Services are free," no one turned away) — [SAFE Austin](https://safeaustin.org/our-services/face-to-face-support/survivor-peer-support)
- Accepting VAWA/FVPSA/VOCA funds brings the confidentiality obligations above, including no sharing of PII with funders — [TechSafety](https://www.techsafety.org/vawa-privacy-and-effective-oversight)
- Mighty Networks charges transaction fees, which are irrelevant if the community is free but a factor if donations run through it — [SchoolMaker](https://www.schoolmaker.com/blog/mighty-networks-pricing)

### Inferences
**Monetization (ethical)**
- Free for all survivors, permanently. No ads, no Meta or Google pixels, no third-party analytics on community pages. Use privacy-respecting server-side analytics or none.
- Revenue sources: individual donors and recurring giving (on the main site, never inside the community); corporate sponsors acknowledged on the public site only, with no data access and no sponsor messaging in the community; foundation grants (women's funds, tech-safety funders); state DV coalition sub-grants (bring VAWA/VOCA rules); partner DV agencies paying a small fee to refer clients and co-supervise; paid training for corporate HR/ERGs on supporting employees experiencing DV (a separate revenue line).
- Avoid paid tiers for survivors and avoid "premium mentors." Paid mentor stipends are fine and encouraged.

**Phased plan (realistic)**
- **Phase 0 (months 0–3), foundations:** Legal entity or fiscal sponsor, counsel review (terms, privacy, mandated reporting, VAWA posture), insurance quotes. Recruit a clinical advisor (LCSW). Partner with 1–2 DV agencies or the state coalition for referral and training. Write the code of conduct, crisis protocol, data-retention policy and subpoena policy. Do threat modeling with Safety Net resources. Budget estimate (inference): coordinator 0.5 FTE, ~$30–40k; legal ~$5–15k; insurance ~$2–6k/yr; tech ~$1–3k setup.
- **Phase 1 (months 3–6), pilot:** Self-hosted Discourse, invite-only, with 15–30 referred members and 4–6 trained mentors (40-hour training plus atherpace modules). Pre-moderate everything. No DMs except supervised mentor pairs. Run one weekly facilitated Zoom or Jitsi group. Monthly clinical supervision. Measure safety incidents, retention, and member-reported helpfulness via anonymous surveys.
- **Phase 2 (months 6–12), structured mentoring:** Formal 1:1 match program (3–6 month terms) using the Brave Beginnings and MOSAIC models. Open self-referral with an intake call. Add Spanish-language and identity-specific groups (the SAFE Austin model). Second moderator. Publish an aggregate-only impact report with broad categories.
- **Phase 3 (year 2+), scale:** More mentor cohorts, multi-state partner agencies, corporate training revenue. Evaluate with a partner researcher (pre/post wellbeing and safety measures). Consider a mobile app only if safety review supports it.
- **Kill/pause criteria:** Any confirmed abuser infiltration, or supervision coverage lapses. Pause new admissions until reviewed.

- I found no published budgets for comparable programs. The cost figures above are estimates and should be validated with partner agencies.
- I found no source on sponsor-funding norms specific to DV peer communities.
