# Domestic Violence Among Rural Women in the US: Geography, Barriers, Populations, Interventions, Resources

Research date: November 2026. Data years are noted for each item. These notes come from a limited pass of about 20 searches and fetches. Several figures come from search-result summaries rather than full-text reads; those are marked "(snippet-level)".

## 1. Which US states and regions are hardest hit (IPV prevalence and DV homicide of women)? Which are heavily rural?

### Takeaway
No current, official state-by-state ranking exists for women killed by men. The Violence Policy Center (VPC) suspended its state rankings starting with the 2023 data because of the FBI's change in crime reporting. The latest edition (2025, using 2023 data) gives national figures only. Across 25 years of past VPC rankings, Alaska was most often the worst state. NISVS prevalence data by state are old (2016/2017 at the latest), and third-party compilations such as WorldPopulationReview label their years inconsistently. Even so, the states those compilations rank highest are largely rural: Arkansas, Missouri, Alaska, West Virginia, Oklahoma, Maine.

### Cited Findings
**VPC "When Men Murder Women"**
- The 28th edition (released September 2025) analyzes 2023 FBI data on single-victim, single-offender homicides with a female victim and a male offender. It presents national data only: "the information currently available does not allow for state ranking" because of the FBI's ongoing change in how it collects and reports crime data. — [VPC publication page](https://vpc.org/publication/when-men-murder-women-an-analysis-of-2023-homicide-data/); [VPC 2025 PDF](https://vpc.org/studies/wmmw2025.pdf)
- 2023 national findings: about 9 in 10 women murdered by men were killed by someone they knew, and nearly two-thirds died by gunfire (64.9%, per a VPC summary; snippet-level). — [VPC press release](https://vpc.org/nearly-nine-out-of-10-women-murdered-by-men-are-killed-by-someone-they-know-and-two-thirds-die-by-gunfire-new-violence-policy-center-study-finds-2/)
- Black women murdered by men are almost always killed by someone they know, most commonly with a gun (2023 data). — [VPC press release](https://vpc.org/press/black-women-murdered-by-men-are-almost-always-killed-by-someone-they-know-most-commonly-with-a-gun/)
- 25-year retrospective (FBI data 1996–2020, published 2023):
  - more than 45,800 women were killed by men in single-victim/single-offender incidents
  - 92% knew their killer
  - 61% of those with a known offender were killed by an intimate partner
  - firearms were used in 53%
  - Black women consistently had the highest rates
  - the report names firearm relinquishment laws as effective in reducing intimate partner homicide
  — [VAWnet summary of VPC 25-year review](https://vawnet.org/material/when-men-murder-women-review-25-years-female-homicide-victimization-united-states)
- Headline on the 25-year retrospective: "Eight states led by Alaska have topped list of men killing women." The article could not be retrieved (404), so the other seven states are unverified. — [NCJA (title only)](https://www.ncja.org/crimeandjusticenews/eight-states-led-by-alaska-have-topped-list-of-men-killing-women)
- Alaska ranked #1 for several consecutive years in past editions. — [Alaska's News Source, "For fourth year in a row, Alaska has highest rate of men killing women"](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/content/news/Report-For-fourth-year-in-a-row-Alaska-has-highest-rate-of-men-killing-women-560736821.html)
- Nevada has repeatedly ranked high, with guns the most common weapon. — [News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/nevada-rate-of-women-killed-by-men-domestic-sexual-violencee-dv)
- Historically, Alaska, South Carolina, and Oklahoma often topped the list (for example, the 2012 data). This came from a search summary (snippet-level). — [Time, 2014](https://time.com/3312681/women-are-most-likely-to-be-killed-by-a-man-in-these-states); [Ms. Magazine, 2021](https://msmagazine.com/2021/10/27/women-killed-by-men-femicide-gender-domestic-violence/)

**CDC NISVS state prevalence**
- The NISVS 2016/2017 State Report is the most recent state-level release (published around 2023).
  - Women's lifetime intimate partner sexual violence, physical violence, and/or stalking ranges from 31.7% to 61.8% across states.
  - A narrower IPV measure ranges from 22.4% to 43.8%. This came from a garbled snippet, so verify it in the report tables.
  — [PreventConnect](https://www.preventconnect.org/2023/12/national-intimate-partner-and-sexual-violence-survey-2016-2017-state-report-released/); [VAWnet](https://vawnet.org/material/national-intimate-partner-and-sexual-violence-survey-20162017-state-report); [CDC Stacks NISVS 2016/2017 IPV report](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/124646)
- NISVS has no urbanization variable. The National Advisory Committee on Rural Health and Human Services (2015) asked CDC to add one. — [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)

**WorldPopulationReview compilation**
- Lifetime "any domestic violence" among women, labeled 2024 and attributed to CDC NISVS. The page links to the 2016–2017 NISVS State Report, so the year label is unreliable.
  - Top 10: Arkansas 48.1%, Missouri 47.3%, Alaska 47.1%, Oregon 44.2%, West Virginia 44.1%, Maryland 43.3%, Washington 42.2%, Maine 42.0%, Oklahoma 41.6%, Pennsylvania 41.3%.
  - Lowest: New Jersey 21.0%, Florida 24.6%, Connecticut 26.2%, North Dakota 27.1%, Iowa 28.3%.
  — [WorldPopulationReview](https://worldpopulationreview.com/state-rankings/domestic-violence-by-state)

**Native women**
- AI/AN women's murder rate is almost 3 times that of non-Hispanic white women (Petrosky et al., 2017). In Wyoming the rate is 6.4 times higher. — [NCAI research policy update](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/raa5sn1v/production/d8e2741cc91ccc864d72d284e73807cbef7b112c.pdf); [NCAI key statistics](https://ncai.org/section/vawa/overview/key-statistics)

### Inferences
- For the site, frame homicide geography with VPC historical rankings (Alaska consistently worst; South Carolina, Oklahoma, and Nevada frequent) plus a clear note that rankings were suspended after 2022-era data.
- The highest-prevalence states in the WPR/NISVS compilation are largely rural or Appalachian/Ozark/Plains/frontier: Arkansas, Missouri, Alaska, West Virginia, Oklahoma, Maine. Alaska appears high in both prevalence and homicide and has a large AI/AN and remote-village population, so it is the clearest hot spot.
- Regions to feature: Alaska and the Northern Plains/Mountain West tribal lands; Appalachia (WV, KY, TN); the Ozarks/rural South (AR, MO, OK, SC).

### Gaps
- The exact states in the "eight states led by Alaska" retrospective were not retrieved (the source returned 404).
- The VPC 2025 PDF could not be fetched (404), so exact 2023 counts and rates (number of women killed, rate per 100,000) are not confirmed.
- No verified state rural share was compiled. The USDA ERS or Census rural percentages by state would be needed to label states "heavily rural" quantitatively.
- The WPR year labeling conflicts with its linked source. Verify figures against the CDC 2016/2017 State Report tables before publishing.

## 2. Rural vs. urban differences (prevalence, severity, homicide, firearms, distance, shelters, broadband, transportation, anonymity, law enforcement, culture, economics, animals, substance use)

### Takeaway
The best US evidence finds rural women, especially in small and isolated towns, have equal or higher IPV prevalence. They also report more severe and frequent physical abuse, higher IPV-related hospitalization, and higher female/intimate partner homicide risk, often involving guns. They live roughly 3 times farther from services, and the services that exist have fewer shelter beds. Barriers center on distance, transportation, connectivity, lack of anonymity, ties between abusers and local authorities, self-reliance norms, economic dependence, animals, and substance use.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence and severity: Peek-Asa et al. 2011 (Journal of Women's Health; Iowa)**
- Sample: 1,478 women seeking elective abortion at one Iowa clinic, 2007–08.
- 12-month IPV:
  - urban 15.5%
  - small rural towns 22.5% (adjusted OR 1.61)
  - isolated rural 17.9%
- Physical/sexual IPV: 11.6% urban vs. 18.8% small rural.
- Severity:
  - 61.5% of isolated rural victims reported 4 or more physical violence events in the past year, vs. 39.3% of urban victims
  - over 30% of isolated rural victims reported severe or very severe violence, vs. 10% of urban victims
— [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3216064); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21919777/)

**Distance and service capacity (same study)**
- Average distance to the nearest DV program for IPV victims:
  - urban women: 6.5 miles
  - small rural: 29.6 miles
  - isolated rural: 29.4 miles
- Over 25% of small rural and isolated victims lived more than 40 miles from services, vs. under 1% of urban women.
- 71 of Iowa's 99 counties (71.7%) had no DV program in the county.
- Rural programs each served 5.6 counties on average, vs. 2.8 for urban programs.
- On-site shelter at about 44% of remote rural programs vs. over 75% elsewhere.
- Shelter beds per county: 13.8 urban vs. 3.3 remote rural.
— [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3216064)

**Hospitalization and homicide**
- IPV prevalence may be similar in rural and urban areas, but IPV-related hospitalizations are greater in rural areas (2020 policy brief on emergency department visits, 2009–2014). — [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)
- Rural pregnant and postpartum residents had higher IPV prevalence and were less likely to be screened (2023 study). — [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)
- "Areas with few services are also associated with higher levels of IPV-related homicide" (2020 brief). — [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)
- A US study cited by Strand & Storey reported intimate partner homicide rates of 8.3 per 100,000 in rural areas vs. 2.0 in metropolitan areas (1980–1999 data; secondhand, snippet-level). — [Strand & Storey 2019](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/intimate-partner-violence-in-urban-rural-and-remote-areas-an-inve/)
- Female homicide rates were higher in rural places (2005–2017 data, 16 states; Journal of Rural Health, DOI 10.1111/jrh.12670). — cited in [DeKeseredy, The Conversation / Mississippi Free Press, Sept 8, 2025](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/)
- NCVS 1992–2005: rural women report physical and sexual violence at higher rates than women in densely populated areas (DeKeseredy, Rennison & Dragiewicz; DOI 10.1177/1077801213514487). — [same](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/)
- Gender equality is a protective factor against intimate partner homicide in rural North Carolina counties (Gillespie & Reckdenwald 2017, Feminist Criminology 12(2)). — [Boise State ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.boisestate.edu/crimjust_facpubs/200)
- Context: 34% of female murder victims in 2021 were killed by an intimate partner (BJS). — [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)

**Firearms**
- 46% of rural adults own guns vs. 19% in urban places (Pew 2017). Firearms were used in 54% of rural domestic homicides (no source given for the 54%).
- Background checks for all handgun owners are associated with about 38% fewer women shot by intimate partners.
- Extending gun bans to people under temporary restraining orders is associated with about 14% lower intimate partner homicide (DOI 10.1017/jme.2023.41).
— [DeKeseredy 2025](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/)
- A 2020 study in the International Journal of Rural Criminology examines intimate partner femicide by firearm type across the rural/urban divide. — [OSU Knowledge Bank](https://kb.osu.edu/items/0c396535-fcaf-4044-918d-f356adc75a97)
- Overall gun homicide (not IPV-specific) is lower in rural counties on average (3.5 per 100,000) than in large metro counties (5.1) (CDC data through 2024). This is a caution against overgeneralizing. — [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-highest-rates-of-gun-homicides-are-in-rural-counties/)

**Structural and cultural barriers (RHIhub topic guide)**
- Distance to clinics and shelters, and no public transit.
- Lack of broadband and reliable phone service.
- Few shelters, little affordable housing, and difficulty keeping shelter locations confidential.
- Little anonymity: abusers may share social networks with providers.
- Abusers may be tied to local law enforcement or others in authority, so survivors fear they won't be protected or kept confidential.
- Self-reliance norms and resistance to outside intervention.
- Economic dependence and few relocation resources.
- Limited DV training in the health workforce.
- Rural emergency departments in Oregon had fewer screening tools, less onsite advocacy, and less staff training than urban ones.
- Rural women granted protective orders were more likely to fear future harm (NIJ). Reasons include isolation, few services, more marriages to the respondent, and more shared children.
— [RHIhub Violence and Abuse in Rural America](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)
- Crime in rural areas is reported to police at lower rates. — [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse), via [DeKeseredy 2025](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/)

**Animals**
- 97% of survivors say keeping pets with them is important in deciding whether to seek shelter, and 48% worried the abuser would harm or kill pets (Urban Resource Institute / National DV Hotline survey).
- Only about 3% of US DV shelters (about 250) offer co-living with pets. Other estimates of shelters that accept pets run 15–17%; the definitions differ.
- 52% of sheltered survivors left pets with the abuser (2004 study, dated).
— [AVMA/JAVMA 2021](https://www.avma.org/javma-news/2021-11-01/report-shows-need-more-pet-friendly-domestic-violence-shelters); [RedRover](https://redrover.org/dvhelp/); [Fetch Pet](https://www.fetchpet.com/the-dig/pet-shelters-domestic-violence)

**Substance use (Appalachia)**
- Among 114 pregnant women entering inpatient detox at the University of Kentucky, from rural Appalachian Kentucky, 75.3% had past-year IPV. This is a clinical sample (snippet-level). — [PMC8628311](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/35a874ebfd05ff498567574cdb04b1e1/PMC8628311.pdf)
- Among 488 pregnant women at five prenatal clinics in South-Central Appalachia, IPV had a direct association with opioid use, not moderated by rurality (Substance Use & Misuse, 2022). — [ETSU](https://etsu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/intimate-partner-violence-victimization-and-opioid-use-by-pregnan/); [Buffalo](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/prevalence-of-opioid-use-and-intimate-partner-violence-among-preg/)
- Recent partner violence victims in rural Appalachia had higher lifetime nonmedical opiate and sedative use than the state population (2011 SSWR abstract). — [SSWR 2011](https://sswr.confex.com/sswr/2011/webprogram/Paper14823.html)

### Inferences
- Messaging for the site: rural survivors are not necessarily abused more often everywhere, but abuse is more severe, help is roughly 30 or more miles away, and guns raise lethality. Safety planning should center firearms, distance, and confidentiality.
- Livestock and farm animals: no data found (see Gaps). Pet-related findings likely understate the barrier for farm and ranch women, who may be responsible for many animals and for their livelihood.

### Gaps
- No nationally representative US rural/urban IPV prevalence estimate was found. NISVS lacks a rurality variable.
- No data were found on:
  - law enforcement response times for rural DV
  - broadband gap figures specific to survivors
  - livestock or farm animals in DV
- The figure "firearms used in 54% of rural domestic homicides" has no primary source traced.

## 3. Specific populations: AI/AN women on tribal lands, Appalachia, the rural South, migrant farmworker women, older rural women

### Takeaway
American Indian and Alaska Native women face the most extreme burden:
- 84% lifetime violence
- 97% of victims experienced violence by a non-Native perpetrator, which creates jurisdictional gaps that VAWA 2013 and 2022 only partially fix
- a murder rate about 3 times that of white women (6.4 times in Wyoming)

Appalachian evidence links IPV with opioid use. Farmworker women face very high workplace sexual harassment, but no farmworker-specific IPV prevalence data were found.

### Cited Findings
**AI/AN women (NIJ 2016)**
- 84.3% of AI/AN women experienced violence in their lifetime, and 39.8% in the past year.
- 97% of female victims experienced violence by a perpetrator of a different race. Tribes historically lacked jurisdiction over non-Indian offenders. VAWA 2013 partially corrected this through special domestic violence criminal jurisdiction.
- Nearly half of female victims needed services, and 38% of those could not get them.
— [NIJ](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/violence-against-american-indian-and-alaska-native-women-and-men-0); [NCJRS PDF](https://www.ncjrs.gov/pdffiles1/nij/249822.pdf); [NCAI](https://www.ncai.org/section/vawa/overview/key-statistics)

**MMIW**
- AI/AN women's murder rate is about 3 times that of non-Hispanic white women, and 6.4 times in Wyoming. — [NCAI update](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/raa5sn1v/production/d8e2741cc91ccc864d72d284e73807cbef7b112c.pdf)
- GAO (2021, GAO-22-104045): the total number of missing or murdered Indigenous women is unknown because federal databases lack comprehensive data. — [GAO](https://gao.gov/products/gao-22-104045)
- Savanna's Act (2020) requires DOJ to report statistics on missing or murdered AI/AN people. — [DOJ report](https://www.justice.gov/media/1398381/dl); [NPR 2020](https://www.npr.org/sections/live-updates-protests-for-racial-justice/2020/09/28/917807372/savannas-act-addresses-alarming-numbers-of-missing-or-murdered-native-women)
- The often-repeated claim that Native women are murdered at "10 times the national average" on some reservations was found only in unsourced secondary pieces. Do not use it without a primary source. — [Campbell Law Observer](https://campbelllawobserver.com/missing-and-murdered-indigenous-women-an-epidemic/)

**StrongHearts Native Helpline**
- 24/7 call or text at 1-844-7NATIVE (1-844-762-8483), plus chat at strongheartshelpline.org.
- Free, anonymous, and confidential, serving AI/AN people affected by domestic or sexual violence.
- Text advocacy launched in March 2021.
— [StrongHearts terms](https://strongheartshelpline.org/about/terms-and-conditions); [ACF hotlines](https://acf.gov/ofvps/programs-services/hotlines-helplines); [SDRUM 2021](https://www.sudrum.com/health/2021/03/26/stronghearts-native-helpline-launches-text-advocacy/)

**Appalachia**
- Studies link IPV and opioid use (see section 2).
- Researchers attribute heightened risk to poverty, distrust of outsiders, geographic isolation, and high interpersonal violence.
- A study of 400 rural, drug-using Appalachian women recruited from jails found heterogeneous risk profiles (SSWR 2019, snippet-level).
— [SSWR 2019](https://sswr.confex.com/sswr/2019/webprogram/Paper36395.html)
- DeKeseredy (West Virginia University) argues rural women are more likely than urban and suburban women to be killed by a current or former male partner. — [DeKeseredy 2025](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/)

**Latina and migrant farmworker women**
- 2007 study of 292 Latina women in San Diego County: lifetime physical IPV 33.9%, sexual coercion 20.9%, psychological aggression 82.5%. Migrant seasonal workers were more likely to report past-year sexual coercion. — [NIJ/OJP](https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/experiences-intimate-partner-violence-among-latina-women)
- About 80% of 150 Mexican immigrant farmworking women in California reported workplace sexual harassment (2010, Violence Against Women). — [OJP abstract](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/examining-sexual-harassment-experiences-mexican-immigrant)
- 75% of 20 women in Yakima Valley focus groups shared harassment stories (UW thesis 2014). — [UW](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/handle/1773/27163)
- Research exists on rural DV agencies' challenges serving Latinx survivors. — [RHIhub resources](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse/resources)

### Inferences
- A tribal-lands subsection should explain jurisdiction plainly and lead with StrongHearts. It should treat the 84% NIJ figure and the 3x homicide rate as the anchor statistics.
- Content for farmworker women should address immigration-status fears and Spanish and Indigenous-language access. This is inferred from the population; no study was found that measures it.

### Gaps
- No rural-specific research was found on IPV among older women (elder abuse by partners).
- No farmworker-specific IPV prevalence estimate was found.
- No rural South-specific IPV dataset was found beyond the state rankings.
- The current DOJ Savanna's Act report figures were not retrieved.

## 4. Interventions and resources that work

### Takeaway
The evidence base is mostly program descriptions, not trials. Recommended rural models are:
- mobile advocacy
- telehealth, text, and chat advocacy
- coordinated community response
- health care–DV partnerships and medical-legal partnerships
- transportation
- pet safekeeping (RedRover, Safe Havens for Pets)
- faith-leader partnerships (the Hearts and Hands toolkit)
- libraries as access points
- firearm-removal laws, which have the strongest quantitative evidence for reducing intimate partner homicide

Federal funding flows through OVW's Rural Program: 56 awards totaling $38.38M in FY2025.

### Cited Findings
**RHIhub-listed models**
- Mobile advocacy: advocates meet survivors at a secure place of their choosing, with 24/7 in-person, text, or phone support.
- Telehealth links to counselors.
- Coordinated community response.
- The IPV Health Partners toolkit and the CUES approach.
- Medical-legal partnerships.
- Batterer intervention programs (Duluth Model, Emerge).
- Program examples: Massachusetts TeleSANE Center, Kingsville End Domestic Violence Task Force (Texas), a rural Texas support network.
— [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse)
- In Iowa, 100% of rural DV programs offered transportation, vs. 52% of urban programs. — [Peek-Asa 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3216064)

**OVW Rural Program**
- Purpose: enhance the safety of rural victims of sexual assault, DV, dating violence, and stalking.
- Authority: 34 U.S.C. § 12341; Assistance Listing 16.589.
- FY2025: 56 awards totaling $38.38M. The pre-award estimate was 55 awards worth $500K–$950K each, about $36M total.
— [DOJ OVW Rural Program](https://www.justice.gov/ovw/rural-program); [FY2025 NOFO](https://simpler.grants.gov/opportunity/358847); [Montana copy](https://mbcc.mt.gov/_docs/Funding/Other-Funding-Opportunities/OVW_FY25-Rural_FINAL.pdf)

**Faith leaders**
- "Hearts and Hands" (2013) is a toolkit from the Safe Havens Interfaith Partnership Against Domestic Violence. It supports partnerships between rural faith leaders and DV victim services. — [RHIhub resource](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/resources/6599)

**Pets**
- RedRover Safe Escape grants cover pet boarding for up to 45 days while a survivor is in shelter.
- RedRover offers grants of up to $60,000 for shelters to become pet-friendly.
- The "25 by 2025" campaign is run by Greater Good Charities with PetSmart Charities.
- The Safe Havens for Pets directory is searchable by ZIP code.
— [RedRover](https://redrover.org/dvhelp/); [Kong/RedRover](https://www.kongcompany.com/how-redrover-saves-lives-at-both-ends-of-the-leash); [DomesticShelters.org](https://www.domesticshelters.org/domestic-violence-articles-information/ask-amanda-what-to-do-with-pets)

**Libraries**
- Proposed uses:
  - library referral to legal aid and DV websites
  - attorney office hours in rural libraries
  - information kiosks
  - private spaces for conversations
  - help with transportation to shelters
- Also proposed: rural safe houses and engaging rural men through town halls.
- These are an expert's recommendations, not evaluated programs.
— [DeKeseredy 2025](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/)

**Firearm policy**
- Universal handgun background checks are associated with about 38% fewer intimate partner firearm femicides.
- Temporary restraining order firearm bans are associated with about 14% lower intimate partner homicide.
— [DOI 10.1017/jme.2023.41 via DeKeseredy](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/opinion-rural-women-are-at-a-higher-risk-of-violence-less-likely-to-get-help/); [VPC 25-yr review](https://vawnet.org/material/when-men-murder-women-review-25-years-female-homicide-victimization-united-states)

**National resources to list**
- National Domestic Violence Hotline: 24/7 call, text, or chat at 1-800-799-7233. The number is from general knowledge; the hotline itself is cited by RHIhub.
- Love Is Respect: ages 13–26.
- StrongHearts Native Helpline: 1-844-762-8483.
— [RHIhub](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse); [ACF OFVPS hotlines](https://acf.gov/ofvps/programs-services/hotlines-helplines)
- RHIhub's Violence and Abuse topic guide and resource library. — [RHIhub resources](https://ruralhealthinfo.org/topics/violence-and-abuse/resources)

### Inferences
- The site's rural section could organize help by access mode:
  - text or chat (where there is cell signal but no privacy)
  - mobile advocates who come to you
  - pet and livestock plans
  - firearm-removal and protective order steps
  - trusted local touchpoints such as libraries, clinics, and faith leaders

### Gaps
- No outcome evaluations were found for:
  - mobile advocacy in rural areas
  - circuit-riding advocates
  - hairstylist or salon training programs (for example, Illinois's 2017 cosmetologist DV training law; not verified here)
  - veterinarian or Farm Bureau training
  - remote or virtual protective order hearings
- NCADV state fact sheets and the state coalition directory were not retrieved; the URLs were not verified this pass. NNEDV maintains a state coalition directory (not verified).
- The National Domestic Violence Hotline number was not verified from a fetched source this pass.
- Livestock-specific safekeeping programs, such as those run by county fairgrounds or extension offices, were not found.
