What is EMDR therapy?

EMDR stands for Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing therapy. Originally created by Francine Shapiro to treat trauma, today it is used to treat a wide variety of issues.

Unlike some therapies that are very cognitive-based, EMDR takes advantage of our brain’s built-in healing network, called the Adaptive Information Processing (AIP) network, to heal maladaptive or dysfunctional networks.

This is the same healing network our brain uses during our deepest cycle of sleep to heal and regenerate, just like any other organ in the body. In EMDR, certain eye movements are used to activate this network and direct it toward the brain networks that are causing difficulty.

What can EMDR help with?

EMDR requires extensive training to practice and can be used to treat a wide variety of issues, including:

  • Trauma / PTSD
  • Depression
  • Anxiety
  • Addiction / substance use
  • Behavioral issues
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder
  • Eating disorders
  • Dissociative disorders
  • And more

Why work with a certified EMDR therapist?

Our founder, Brian (Rob) Robertson, is an EMDRIA-certified EMDR therapist, a credential that reflects advanced training and supervised experience beyond basic EMDR training. That depth matters, especially when working with complex trauma and dissociation.
