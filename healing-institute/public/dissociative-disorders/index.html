Dissociative Disorders

What is a dissociative disorder?

A dissociative disorder is an ongoing condition where a person’s consciousness becomes detached from certain experiences, thoughts, emotions, memories, sensations, or even aspects of their identity. Most dissociative disorders are caused by childhood trauma and abuse, starting early in childhood.

Dissociative disorders were once thought to be rare, but research over the last 20 years estimates that roughly 10% of the general population has a dissociative disorder. You are not alone.

What are some possible symptoms?

It depends on the specific dissociative disorder, but some common symptoms include:

  • Amnesia or poor memory of childhood
  • Feeling outside of your body
  • Difficulty recognizing yourself in the mirror
  • Temporary loss of knowledge
  • Blackouts (not caused by drugs, alcohol, or medications)
  • Thoughts, emotions, impulses, or behaviors that don’t feel like yours

This is not an all-inclusive list. Dissociative disorders are very complex and difficult to detect. A diagnosis should only come from a professional who specializes in this area, as it requires extensive testing.

What are the treatment options?

Since dissociative disorders almost always stem from early childhood trauma, the best treatment options are trauma-focused, as treating the trauma treats the dissociative disorder.

Two of the most common trauma treatments for dissociative disorders are Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) therapy and Brainspotting. Because treating dissociative disorders also involves parts work, incorporating Internal Family Systems (IFS) or ego state therapy is usually needed.

  • EMDR therapy
  • Brainspotting
  • Internal Family Systems (IFS)
  • Ego State Therapy
  • Somatic therapy
  • Clinical hypnosis

Other approaches, such as somatic therapy or hypnosis, can be useful for treating other aspects of dissociative disorders.

If you think you may be experiencing a dissociative disorder, please reach out to us to see how we can help.
