Clinical Hypnosis

Not stage tricks, and never about losing control. Clinical hypnosis is a calm, focused state that can open new insight and support healing, especially for mind-body symptoms.

What is hypnosis?

Humans move through different states of trance all the time. When we are absorbed in a story or a TV show, we are in a state of trance. When we are deep in concentration, or relaxed, we are in trance. These everyday trance phenomena happen all around us.

Hypnosis uses a variety of techniques that take advantage of these normal states to create deep focus and relaxation. This can help us make insights and connections we couldn’t see before, or resolve blocks we couldn’t move past.

Hypnosis is not a stand-alone therapy. It is usually combined with other approaches, such as CBT or EMDR.

What can hypnosis help with?

Mental health conditions

  • Depression
  • Anxiety
  • Panic attacks
  • Phobias
  • PTSD
  • Eating disorders like binge eating

Mind-body and physical conditions

  • Chronic pain
  • Insomnia
  • Fibromyalgia
  • Functional neurological disorder
  • Psychogenic non-epileptic seizures
  • Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
  • Migraines
  • Nausea
  • And more

Common questions & misconceptions

Will I lose control?

No. Hypnosis isn’t about controlling someone or taking control. If anything, hypnosis gives you more control than you thought possible. During hypnosis, you can bypass certain maladaptive mechanisms that may be getting in your way, such as harsh self-judgment, paralyzing fear, indecisiveness, or the inability to observe something without becoming overwhelmed.

For example, someone might seek hypnosis to overcome an addiction like smoking or binge eating. Those habits often feel like they control us, but hypnosis can help us take that control back.

Will I do things I don’t want to do?

No. In hypnosis, it is impossible to get someone to do something they don’t want to do. Real hypnosis is not like what you see on TV or at entertainment shows. Clinical hypnosis isn’t about “doing.” It’s about calming the mind and creating a state of focus that allows you to gain new thoughts, insights, perspectives, and abilities that weren’t available before.

Can anyone perform hypnosis?

Hypnosis should only be performed by a trained professional, especially for clinical applications. Learning clinical hypnosis takes a lot of work and training. It is a difficult skill to learn, even for professionals.

Can anyone be hypnotized?

This is often debated in the clinical community. Some people are more hypnotizable and some are less. People who are more hypnotizable tend to experience more hypnotic phenomena in everyday life. These may include people with PTSD, dissociative disorders, ADHD, or very imaginative thinkers. People who are less hypnotizable are generally very analytical or literal thinkers.

That doesn’t mean less hypnotizable people can’t benefit. There is usually some preparation in therapy to make it easier. What matters most is good therapeutic rapport and a desire for hypnosis to work.

Is hypnosis safe?

In general, hypnosis is considered very safe when conducted by a trained clinical professional. You don’t lose consciousness or free will, and you can’t get “stuck” in hypnosis. If you’ve ever meditated, you’ve probably been in a similar state before. The risks are usually no different from any other form of therapy.

As a precaution, we always leave at least 5–10 minutes at the end of a session to debrief on what you experienced and learned, and to make sure you are fully alert and oriented. As always, discuss any questions or concerns with your provider beforehand.

What does hypnosis therapy look like?

Hypnosis is tailored to the individual. You and your therapist work together on a plan beforehand, answer any questions, and discuss any concerns. Hypnosis is not a standalone therapy and is often used in conjunction with other therapies.

If you want to find out more about how hypnosis might benefit you, please reach out to us.
