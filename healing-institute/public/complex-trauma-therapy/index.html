Complex Trauma & C-PTSD

When harm happened again and again, often beginning in childhood, its effects can echo for years. Healing is possible, with care that understands the whole picture.

What is complex trauma?

While there is no concrete definition, most clinicians agree that complex trauma describes someone having multiple traumatic events in their lifetime, with the first starting in childhood through either childhood abuse or neglect. Some examples of childhood abuse and neglect are physical abuse, sexual abuse, witnessing other forms of violence in childhood, and severe neglect.

There is also usually some element of attachment injury or attachment wounding that happens with complex trauma as well.

What are the signs of complex trauma?

In addition to the typical symptoms of PTSD, such as flashbacks, hypervigilance and physical reactions to memories, someone with complex trauma may also experience:

  • Dissociation
  • Mood instability
  • Emotional numbing
  • Significant reactions to perceived abandonment

It is also possible for someone to have few to no PTSD symptoms at all, especially if there is a lot of dissociative amnesia around childhood memories. While most people with complex trauma remember some of the events, others may have no recollection at all due to dissociative amnesia.

If you don’t remember much of your childhood, that doesn’t mean your experiences aren’t real or valid. Memory gaps can themselves be a way the mind protected you.

What are the impacts of complex trauma?

Complex trauma can affect us long after the trauma is over. Part of our brain is aware that the trauma has ended, but another part is still stuck in what clinicians refer to as “trauma time.”

This can affect our emotional well-being, our functioning at school, work, or in daily life, and our relationships with others. Because complex trauma often includes attachment injury or wounding, it can impact our ability to make and keep relationships.

Complex trauma can also play a part in physiological problems. The Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) study found significant links between complex trauma and physical health, even in adulthood.

What are the treatments for complex trauma / C-PTSD?

There are many effective treatments for resolving complex trauma:

  • EMDR Therapy
  • Brainspotting
  • Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)
  • Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT)
  • Somatic therapy
  • Hypnosis
  • Internal Family Systems (IFS)
  • Ego State Therapy

EMDR and Brainspotting can be good for directly resolving traumatic events. DBT can help with emotion regulation, problematic behaviors, and building stabilization before directly processing traumatic events. Other therapies help in a variety of ways to resolve trauma.
