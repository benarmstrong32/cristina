What to expect when contacting The Hotline ↗

Ask about privacy.

“What information do you need? Who can see it? Are there situations when you have to make a report?” Policies and reporting duties vary.

Ask about the help you need.

“Do you offer this service? Is there a cost or a wait? Can you help if I’m staying where I am?”

Ask about access.

“Is an interpreter available? Can you support my disability or access needs? Are there options for my children or pets?”

If this isn’t the right fit.

You can ask for another referral. Limited space, eligibility rules, or a long wait do not mean your needs matter less.

How this directory works ↗
What if I’m not sure it’s abuse?

You can read about harmful behaviors or contact The Hotline with questions. You don’t have to choose a label to explore information here.

Do I have to be ready to leave?

No. You can browse information while staying, preparing to leave, after leaving, or while deciding what you want. A trained advocate can help you think through your situation.

Will contacting someone mean a police report?

Different services have different confidentiality and reporting rules. Before sharing details, you can ask what would require them to report information and whether you can ask questions without giving your name. This directory cannot promise what an outside provider will do.

Does free information mean every service is free?

No. This directory and the linked information are free to access. Services, legal representation, transport, and accommodation can have costs, eligibility rules, or limited availability. Ask each provider before making arrangements.

Can I get help without staying in a shelter?

Some local programs offer services beyond shelter, such as advocacy or support groups. The services available differ. You can ask what they offer to people who are not staying in a shelter.

What if I’m worried about children, pets, or immigration?

These are valid things to ask about. The Hotline’s provider directory includes service filters such as pets, interpretation, and Native or Deaf services. WomensLaw also has immigration information. You can ask a provider about privacy before explaining your situation.

A clearer route to existing help

Less searching.
More understandable options.

At Her Pace exists to make free-to-access resources easier for women to find, especially when searching already feels like a lot.

What you can do here

Choose a state and a broad need, browse a few starting points, or read without contacting anyone. Every state has links to local program listings, a statewide support network or government victim-services agency, and legal information.

What this project provides

This is an independently curated directory. Its creator is a resource curator, not a licensed clinician, lawyer, or credentialed domestic violence advocate. It shares general information and links to established organizations. It does not provide therapy, legal advice, individualized safety assessments, case management, emergency response, or crisis conversations.

Your story belongs to you

This site has no personal-information intake or sign-in requirement. There is no form for personal disclosures and no chatbot. The state finder uses only the state and broad need you select, within your browser, without saving those choices.

How resources are chosen

We prioritize specialist domestic violence organizations, state networks, government victim-services information, and established public-service directories. We describe the destination and distinguish directories and educational information from direct services. Listings are not paid placements. No referral commissions or donations are collected through this site.

What “reviewed” means

We reviewed primary-source pages or search-indexed primary-site results on October 31, 2026. Some research requests were blocked or rate-limited; where appropriate we used indexed primary results or a confirmed alternative page. This is a source review, not a call to the provider, a service-quality assessment, or confirmation of availability. Each listing shows the date it was last reviewed.

If a link or service doesn’t work for you

You can try The Hotline’s provider directory or WomensLaw’s main resource finder. You can also ask a program to suggest another referral. This site cannot respond to requests for individual help. Please contact one of the organizations listed for support.

Who the directory is for

The directory centers women in the United States, including trans women, and welcomes anyone who finds these links useful. Some resources address partner abuse, while others include family violence, sexual violence, or past abuse. A listing does not mean a program covers every circumstance; ask about its scope and eligibility.

At Her Pace is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by any organization listed here. Organization names are used only to identify and link to their public resources.

Privacy & browsing

Know what this site does.
And what it cannot do.

This directory is designed to ask for as little as possible. It cannot make a monitored device private or promise that a visit will be undetectable.

Effective November 6, 2026.

Summary

At Her Pace does not ask for, collect, sell, or share personal information. There are no accounts, sign-ups, forms, cookies, analytics, or advertising trackers. The resource links lead to external websites.

Information you do not need to submit

The directory has no intake form, visitor account system, story submission, comments, discussion forum, direct messages, chat service, document upload, or newsletter signup. Please do not send personal disclosures through an unrelated account or channel expecting this directory to provide confidential support.

Your state and need selections

The finder uses a fixed list of public resources in the current browser page. Its code does not transmit your chosen state or broad need through a form, store them in cookies or browser storage, or add them to the URL. Clear my selections resets the current page. Hide page also clears those choices and covers the page until you choose to return.

Browsers and devices can have features beyond the control of this site, including history, tab previews, screenshots, autofill, screen recording, and monitoring software. Clear my selections and Hide page do not remove those records or prevent monitoring. Browser autocomplete is requested to be off; that request is not a guarantee.

Hosting and technical records

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. Like most web hosts, GitHub may automatically record technical information such as IP addresses, the pages requested, and access times for security and operations. At Her Pace does not receive or use that information to identify visitors. Your internet provider and your device may also keep records. This site is not anonymous, confidential, or a HIPAA-covered service.

The directory’s own code adds no analytics, advertising trackers, third-party fonts, embedded maps or videos, or application cookies. It does not request geolocation, camera, or microphone access. Read GitHub’s privacy statement.

Hide page and Quick exit are different

Hide page covers this tab with a neutral page and clears the finder’s selections. Someone with access to the device can return to the guide. It is not a lock, password, or secure deletion feature.

Quick exit clears the page choices, covers its content, and tries to replace this tab with a weather website. You can also press Escape twice within one second. The destination uses its own privacy practices and may not load immediately. Quick exit does not erase history, close other tabs, clear account records, or stop screenshots or monitoring. With JavaScript disabled, use your browser’s own controls to leave.

Following a resource link

Links open in the same tab. The site uses a no-referrer policy to reduce what the destination receives about this page, but the destination still receives your request and may record technical data. Other websites control their own forms, cookies, confidentiality rules, and reporting duties. Calls, texts, visits, and downloads may leave records. Ask a service about its practices before sharing details.

What others can see through this directory

The directory publishes public organization and information links. It does not publish visitor profiles, visitor locations, selections, personal stories, confidential shelter street addresses, resident lists, or live shelter occupancy. External directories may publish their own information; this site cannot control their content.

Access cannot establish someone’s intent

A public resource website cannot reliably identify every abusive visitor. Requiring a person to prove abuse or upload identification would create additional sensitive information and could block people who need help. This site does not collect that information. Its protection strategy is to avoid creating personal records or exposing private locations, rather than promising to keep every harmful person away.

Considering safer access

If possible, use a device or account the abusive person cannot access. Private or incognito browsing does not defeat all monitoring. Changes to device settings or attempts to remove monitoring can have consequences; consider discussing your options with a trained advocate.

Accessibility

More ways to use
this guide.

The goal is understandable navigation that works with different devices and ways of browsing. Accessibility is ongoing work, not a guarantee that every person’s needs have already been met.

What this site includes

The guide includes explicit labels, native state and need selectors, visible keyboard focus, a skip-to-content link, text alternatives for the logo, and text announcements when results change. You can zoom using your browser’s controls. Reduced-motion preferences are respected, and the content uses no flashing media or autoplay.

Keyboard access

Use Tab and Shift+Tab to move between controls and links. Use your device’s standard keys to operate a select menu. Hide page clears choices and covers the page; focus moves to its message. Return to resource guide restores the page and keyboard focus to the main content. Quick exit is also available with two presses of Escape within one second when JavaScript is enabled.

Without JavaScript

The guided finder, Hide page, Clear selections, and Quick exit controls require JavaScript. The complete directory for all 50 states and DC is available as ordinary HTML links. Browser navigation remains available. No personal disclosure is required to read the directory.

Alternative sources

If this page does not work for you, try The Hotline’s support options, The Deaf Hotline’s ASL resources, or WomensLaw’s resource finder. Their accessibility and privacy practices are their own.

Ongoing review

Text-color contrast, source markup, internal links, JavaScript syntax, and all 51 state selections have been checked in development. A full assistive-technology, mobile, zoom/reflow, and disability-led usability review has not been completed. The project does not claim ADA certification or full WCAG conformance.

If something on this site is hard to use, please let us know at contact@atherpace.org. You don’t need to share anything about your situation, only what part of the site was difficult.
